Graph Networks and Neural Cellular Automata

Can shared local rules learn a global pattern?

The idea

Message passing gathers neighbour information and updates each node with shared functions. A symmetric aggregation such as summation avoids dependence on the order in which neighbours are listed. Edges and message design determine which interactions the model can represent.

A Neural Cellular Automaton repeatedly applies a learned local update on a grid, often with continuous hidden channels. Training can adjust the update after unrolling many steps towards a target. Growing a pattern, keeping it alive and regenerating damage are separate capabilities requiring separate evidence.

Read the relationship

mᵢ=ΣⱼMθ(hᵢ,hⱼ,eᵢⱼ); hᵢ⁺=Uθ(hᵢ,mᵢ); NCA sketch: hᵢ⁺=hᵢ+Δθ(local perception)

hᵢ is node state, eᵢⱼ an edge feature and θ shared parameters. Shared parameters do not make every node's current state identical.

Work through an example

  1. On a three-node chain use hᵢ⁺=hᵢ+0.25Σⱼ(hⱼ−hᵢ).
  2. From (1,0,0), one update gives (0.75,0.25,0), preserving the total one.
  3. Replacing the hand-designed rule with a trained network does not automatically preserve that total; constraints must be designed or checked.

When it applies

Neighbourhood range, oversmoothing and finite training horizons limit behaviour. A convincing target image does not establish unlimited stability, regeneration or intelligence.

Check your understanding

Does success from one seed prove recovery after any damage?

No. Test unseen damage, different initial states and long runs against explicit criteria.
