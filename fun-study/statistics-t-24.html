Probability and Statistics / 24

A complete project: evidence, prediction, and decisions

What must a report include before another person can trust it?

The idea

A useful analysis starts with a concrete question and a decision time. For a device-failure predictor, specify which readings are available when a prediction is made, how future failure is labeled, and which mistakes are costly. Build a simple baseline before adding complexity so that improvement has a meaningful reference.

A defensible report keeps probability quality, threshold decisions, and uncertainty separate. Record the sampling process, data split, preprocessing, model choices, final evaluation, and failure cases. Examine groups and changing conditions rather than relying only on one overall score. A well-documented negative result can be more useful than an inflated performance claim.

Read the relationship

Brier score=Σ(pᵢ−yᵢ)²/n; average cost=(Cfp·FP+Cfn·FN)/n

pᵢ is a predicted probability, yᵢ is a binary outcome, and FP/FN count the two kinds of decision error.

Work through an example

  1. On 1,000 tests, let TP=30, FP=20, FN=10, and TN=940.
  2. Accuracy is 97%, precision is 30/50=60%, and recall is 30/40=75%.
  3. With false-positive cost one and false-negative cost ten, total cost is 120, or 0.12 per test; always predicting normal costs 0.4 per test.

When it applies

No metric establishes universal suitability. Choose independent evaluation data representative of the intended use and report the evidence's limits.

Check your understanding

Could a lower Brier score alone prove calibration improved?

No. It reflects several aspects of probability prediction; inspect calibration directly and compare against suitable baselines.
