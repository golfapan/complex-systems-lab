Randomness and Regularity / 02

Deterministic trajectories, evolving distributions

How can probability change when every individual trajectory is fixed?

The idea

Imagine repeatedly preparing a system with slightly different initial states. Each state follows its own deterministic trajectory, while the collection forms a distribution. Probability mass moves with the vector field: regions can stretch, compress, or shift even though no new noise is injected along any individual path.

The continuity or Liouville equation expresses that bookkeeping. Its prediction concerns the density of possible states rather than the identity of one trajectory. It therefore bridges differential equations and probability without replacing deterministic dynamics with random forces. Boundary fluxes and the chosen state variables remain part of the model.

Read the relationship

∂tρ+∇·(ρf)=0; for ẋ=ax, Xt=eᵃᵗX₀

ρ is a state density normalized to integrate to one; f is the deterministic velocity field in state space.

Work through an example

  1. Let X₀ have mean μ₀ and variance σ₀², and let every realization obey ẋ=ax.
  2. At time t, the mean is eᵃᵗμ₀ and the variance is e²ᵃᵗσ₀².
  3. With a>0, uncertainty spreads exponentially even though selecting an exact initial value determines its entire trajectory.

When it applies

A density description depends on the initial ensemble. It does not say that a single isolated system contains that entire ensemble physically.

Check your understanding

If variance grows, must fresh random noise be entering the system?

No. Deterministic stretching of an initially uncertain state can increase variance without any continuing stochastic forcing.
