PDEs and Types of Physical Problem

Why can a temperature field need more than a single state variable?

The idea

A field assigns a value to every spatial location. A partial differential equation connects changes across space with changes through time. Initial data describe the starting field; boundary conditions describe its interaction with the edge of the domain. The equation alone is usually not a complete problem.

Diffusion smooths variations, waves propagate disturbances with inertia, and elliptic equations describe spatial balance. Begin with the mechanism and required data. Similar-looking second derivatives do not make all three situations equivalent. Their solution behaviour and useful numerical methods can differ sharply.

Read the relationship

Heat: uₜ=κuₓₓ; wave: uₜₜ=c²uₓₓ; Poisson: −uₓₓ=f

Subscripts denote partial derivatives. κ is diffusivity and c wave speed. A wave problem normally needs initial displacement and initial velocity.

Work through an example

  1. On 0<x<π, fix both ends at zero and start with u(x,0)=sin x; choose κ=1.
  2. Try u=a(t)sin x. Since uₓₓ=−a(t)sin x, the PDE becomes a′=−a.
  3. Thus u=e⁻ᵗsin x: this spatial mode retains its shape while its amplitude decays.

When it applies

Classification depends on coefficients, dimension and boundary conditions. Nonlinear or mixed-type equations need additional analysis, and reversing a smoothing evolution can be ill-conditioned.

Check your understanding

Is recovering an old temperature field from a later one necessarily stable?

No. Diffusion erases fine-scale information; reconstructing it can amplify tiny measurement errors.
