ODEs and State Space

What must you know now to predict what happens next?

The idea

A state collects the information your model needs to continue an evolution. For a moving spring, position alone is not enough: two objects at the same position can have opposite velocities and different futures. Choose x=(q,v) so that both position and velocity are available.

An ordinary differential equation specifies the state's rate of change. Parameters describe the model, inputs drive it, and observations describe what a sensor reveals. These roles must remain separate. A state-space diagram plots possible states, not necessarily positions in physical space; one-dimensional motion can therefore require a two-dimensional state space.

Read the relationship

q̇=v; v̇=−(k/m)q−(c/m)v+u/m; y=q

q is displacement, v velocity, m mass, c damping, k stiffness and u applied force. The measured output y need not reveal every state component.

Work through an example

  1. Take m=1, k=2, c=0.5, q=1, v=−2 and u=3.
  2. The rates are q̇=−2 and v̇=−2+1+3=2.
  3. The object still moves left, but its rightward acceleration is slowing that motion.

When it applies

A unique future requires appropriate existence and uniqueness conditions and specified future inputs. Hidden memory or constraints may require a larger or different state.

Check your understanding

Is position plus velocity always the smallest possible state?

No. It is sufficient for this model; sufficient representations need not be minimal, and a different physical model may require additional variables.
