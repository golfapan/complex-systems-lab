Relativity / 04

Simultaneity and length measurements

Why do observers disagree about which separated events happen together?

The idea

Simultaneity at separated locations is frame-dependent. Two events with zero time difference in one inertial frame can have different times in another. This is a direct consequence of time-space mixing, not merely a delay in receiving light from a distant object. It changes how a moving observer measures a rod's length.

Length requires recording the positions of both endpoints at the same time in the measuring frame. A rod's rest length uses simultaneity in its rest frame; its moving length uses a different pair of endpoint events. Keeping track of those event choices resolves many apparent paradoxes about contraction.

Read the relationship

If Δt=0, Δt′=−γvΔx/c²; a moving rod has L=L₀/γ along its motion

L₀ is rest length. Length contraction concerns coordinate measurements under simultaneity rules, not automatically an object's visual appearance.

Work through an example

  1. Two lab events are simultaneous and separated by 3×10⁸ metres along x.
  2. Using c≈3×10⁸ m/s and v=0.6c gives γ=1.25.
  3. Their transformed time difference is −1.25×0.6 seconds=−0.75 seconds, so they are not simultaneous in that moving frame.

When it applies

Transverse dimensions do not follow the same contraction formula. Photographs also include light-travel and viewing geometry effects.

Check your understanding

Can you measure a moving rod's length by recording one endpoint now and the other much later?

Not without correcting for its motion. The standard length measurement uses simultaneous endpoint positions in that frame.
