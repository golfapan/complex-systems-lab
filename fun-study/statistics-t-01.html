Probability and Statistics / 01

Data, populations, and exploratory analysis

What exactly does a row in your dataset represent?

The idea

A dataset is a record of a sampling process, not a miniature copy of the world. Define the population you care about, the unit being measured, and the quantity you want to estimate before choosing a chart or model. A hundred readings from one sensor do not provide the same information as readings from a hundred independent sensors.

Exploratory analysis looks for shape, missingness, unusual values, and differences between groups. The mean uses every numerical value; the median depends on order. Neither is automatically the right summary. A surprising observation deserves investigation rather than automatic deletion, and cleaning decisions must be documented.

Read the relationship

x̄=Σxᵢ/n; s²=Σ(xᵢ−x̄)²/(n−1)

n is the number of observations; s² estimates population variance under independent, identically distributed sampling.

Work through an example

  1. Response times are 1, 2, 2, 3, and 12 milliseconds.
  2. Their sum is 20, so the mean is 4 ms; the middle ordered value is 2 ms.
  3. The long response pulls the mean upward. Whether it is an error or an important failure requires checking its origin.

When it applies

Selection bias and correlated measurements can undermine later inference even when every arithmetic calculation is correct.

Check your understanding

Does a 90% satisfaction rate among respondents imply 90% satisfaction among everyone invited?

No. Nonrespondents may differ systematically; the respondent percentage describes the responding group unless additional assumptions justify generalization.
