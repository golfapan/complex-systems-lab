Density matrices: mixtures and coherence

How do you describe uncertainty about a quantum preparation?

The idea

A density operator represents both pure states and statistical mixtures. It is positive semidefinite, has trace one, and predicts probabilities through traces with measurement operators. For a pure state, ρ=|ψ⟩⟨ψ|. A mixture averages such operators, rather than adding state amplitudes as if a coherent superposition had been prepared.

Off-diagonal terms in a chosen basis carry coherence relevant to interference in other bases. Their meaning depends on that basis. Two preparations can share the same diagonal probabilities yet differ in off-diagonal terms, producing different outcomes when the measurement basis changes. Purity is one useful summary, not a complete description of a state.

Read the relationship

ρ=½[[1,ηe⁻ⁱφ],[ηeⁱφ,1]]; P(+)=(1+ηcosφ)/2; Tr(ρ²)=(1+η²)/2

Here 0≤η≤1 controls coherence in this family and φ is a relative phase. These restrictions ensure a physical density matrix.

Work through an example

  1. Set φ=0 and η=1. The state is |+⟩ and a plus/minus measurement gives plus with certainty.
  2. Set η=0 instead. The state is an equal mixture, giving plus and minus with probability one half.
  3. Both preparations still give equal zero/one probabilities, showing why one basis alone is insufficient.

When it applies

Coherence is basis-dependent, and a reduced mixed state need not arise from ignorance about a globally pure state in a classical sense.

Check your understanding

What are the purities at η=1 and η=0?

They are one and one half, respectively. For this qubit family they distinguish a pure state from the maximally mixed state.
