Balancing an inverted pendulum

How can feedback keep a naturally unstable position upright?

Run this experiment →

The idea

Near the upright position, gravity pushes the pendulum away from balance. A controller applies a restoring torque based on angle error and angular speed. The position term corrects the lean; the speed term can reduce overshoot. Actuator limits can prevent the requested correction from being applied.

A negative-feedback formula is not automatically stabilizing: gains, inertia, gravity and torque limits must work together.

Read the relationship

Iθ̈=m g L sinθ−cθ̇+τ τ=clip(−Kpθ−Kdθ̇,−τmax,τmax)

Here θ=0 is upright, so gravity has the destabilizing sign. I=mL² for the point-mass model.

Try this

  1. Reduce proportional gain until balancing fails. Restore it, then reduce damping gain and compare overshoot.

When it applies

This is a fixed-pivot pendulum with a directly applied joint torque. It is not a cart-pole, and local balancing does not establish global recovery.
