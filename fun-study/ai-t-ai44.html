Uncertainty, Calibration and New Inputs

When should a prediction's confidence be believed?

The idea

Calibration asks whether stated probabilities agree with observed frequencies. Among predictions assigned confidence near p, the corresponding outcome should occur about p of the time under the relevant evaluation conditions. This differs from simply predicting many labels correctly.

Data noise and limited model knowledge are different uncertainty sources. Ensembles, Bayesian approximations and selective prediction offer approaches with assumptions, not universal confidence meters. A distribution shift can invalidate calibration established on familiar data, so examine the new setting directly.

Read the relationship

Calibration idea: P(correct | predicted confidence≈p)≈p

The event being predicted, probability bins and test population must be defined. Accuracy and coverage answer additional questions.

Work through an example

  1. Collect 100 predictions each reporting about 90% confidence.
  2. If only 60 are correct, the group is substantially overconfident.
  3. A refusal rule may improve accuracy among answered cases, but report the fraction rejected as well.

When it applies

A small sample gives noisy calibration estimates. Confidence from fluent language is not necessarily a measured probability.

Check your understanding

Does high accuracy guarantee calibrated probabilities?

No. A model can be often right while systematically overestimating its certainty.
