Automatic Differentiation and Gradient Checks

How can you test a derivative produced by a program?

The idea

Automatic differentiation combines derivatives of elementary program operations using the chain rule. It is distinct from symbolic algebra and from approximating derivatives by perturbing inputs. Reverse mode is especially useful when many parameters feed one scalar loss.

Finite differences provide an independent check on a small, deterministic calculation. The perturbation must balance truncation and rounding errors; at a nonsmooth point, the derivative may not exist. Check several parameters and step sizes rather than accepting one favourable comparison.

Read the relationship

gFD=[L(w+ε)−L(w−ε)]/(2ε)

ε is a small finite perturbation. The finite-difference estimate approximates a derivative and should be compared with the derivative of the same objective.

Work through an example

  1. Use L(w)=(2w−2)²/2 at w=0.5.
  2. Its analytic derivative is 4w−4=−2.
  3. Central differences with ε=10⁻⁵ give approximately −2; severing a graph dependency would typically break that agreement.

When it applies

Random layers, mixed precision and adaptive computations can complicate a comparison. Disable irrelevant randomness and avoid interpreting an infinitesimal perturbation as automatically more accurate.

Check your understanding

Can one successful loss-decreasing update certify all gradients?

No. Directional improvement is weaker than agreement with independent derivative checks across the relevant variables.
