GANs and Adversarial Generation

How can two changing networks teach each other?

The idea

A generative adversarial network couples a generator, which creates samples, with a discriminator, which distinguishes generated from real data. The generator changes in response to the discriminator, while the discriminator learns from the generator's evolving mistakes.

This is a moving game rather than one fixed supervised target. Training stability, sample realism and diversity must all be checked. A generator can produce attractive examples while missing large parts of the target distribution, and a single discriminator loss cannot summarise all those properties.

Read the relationship

min_G max_D Edata log D(x)+Ez log(1−D(G(z)))

G turns random latent input z into a sample; D returns a real-versus-generated score under this original formulation.

Work through an example

  1. Suppose the real dataset contains equally many circles and triangles.
  2. A generator producing only excellent circles may create convincing individual samples.
  3. It still misses half the intended modes, so diversity and coverage tests are essential.

When it applies

The displayed objective is one GAN formulation. Alternative losses and regularisers change training behaviour; apparent visual quality is not an exhaustive distributional test.

Check your understanding

Would a gallery of five excellent samples establish full distribution coverage?

No. It can hide mode collapse or cherry-picking; sample selection and diversity need transparent evaluation.
