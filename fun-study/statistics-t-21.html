PCA, SVD, and clustering

What structure can you find without supplying outcome labels?

The idea

Principal component analysis finds orthogonal directions of greatest variation in centered data. Singular value decomposition provides a matrix representation that makes those directions and the strength of each mode explicit. The resulting coordinates can compress data and expose structure, but high variance is not the same as relevance to a prediction task.

Clustering asks a different question: which observations should be grouped together under a chosen similarity rule? K-means minimizes squared distances to cluster centers. Its results depend on scaling, cluster count, initialization, and whether compact roughly spherical groups are a useful description. It does not automatically discover natural categories.

Read the relationship

X=UΣVᵀ; PCA maximizes vᵀSv subject to ‖v‖=1; K-means minimizes Σ‖xᵢ−ccluster(i)‖²

X is centered data, S its covariance matrix, and singular values in Σ describe retained variation.

Work through an example

  1. Suppose a covariance matrix has eigenvalues nine and one.
  2. The first component explains 9/(9+1)=90% of the total variance.
  3. If the low-variance direction determines the label, dropping it may still harm classification, despite the impressive retained percentage.

When it applies

Fit scaling, PCA directions, and cluster centers on training data only. Units and the desired interpretation should guide standardization.

Check your understanding

Does the first principal component have to be the most important causal mechanism?

No. PCA orders observed variation, not causal importance or predictive usefulness.
