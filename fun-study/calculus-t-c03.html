Using Derivatives to Reason and Optimise

How can local slope information answer questions about a whole design?

The idea

Derivatives help locate increasing and decreasing regions, stationary points and changes of curvature. Finding f′=0 only produces a candidate: x², −x² and x³ all have zero derivative at the origin but behave differently. Boundaries and points where derivatives fail can be just as important.

The mean value theorem links an interval's average slope to a derivative somewhere inside it. Newton's method uses a different consequence of local linearity: extend the current tangent to the horizontal axis and use its intercept as the next guess. Fast convergence near a suitable root is not a universal guarantee.

Read the relationship

f(b)−f(a)=f′(ξ)(b−a), a<ξ<b xₙ₊₁=xₙ−f(xₙ)/f′(xₙ)

ξ is a point whose existence is guaranteed under the theorem's conditions. Newton's denominator must be nonzero at each step.

Work through an example

  1. A rectangle has fixed perimeter P. Write its sides as x and P/2−x, with 0≤x≤P/2.
  2. Its area A=x(P/2−x) has A′=P/2−2x, giving x=P/4.
  3. Because A″=−2 and the endpoints have zero area, the square gives the global maximum P²/16.

When it applies

The mean value theorem needs continuity on the closed interval and differentiability inside. Newton iterations can diverge, especially with poor starting points or tiny derivatives.

Check your understanding

Is f′(a)=0 enough to call a a minimum?

No. Inspect nearby behaviour, curvature where decisive, and the feasible domain; a stationary point can also be a maximum or a saddle-like turning behaviour.
