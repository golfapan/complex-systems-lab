Mixture of Experts and Sparse Routing

How can a model have many parameters but use only some per token?

The idea

A mixture-of-experts layer routes an input to a small subset of expert networks. This often replaces selected feed-forward components. Total parameters describe stored capacity, while active parameters describe only part of the per-token computation.

Routing brings its own costs: selecting experts, balancing load, respecting capacity and moving data between devices. Different tokens may use different experts. Compare end-to-end quality and resource usage rather than treating expert sparsity as a free proportional reduction of all model cost.

Read the relationship

y=Σᵢ∈TopK gᵢ(x)Eᵢ(x)

Eᵢ is expert i and gᵢ its routing weight. The displayed sparse sum describes selected experts, not necessarily every component of the complete network.

Work through an example

  1. Imagine eight equally sized experts and selecting two for each token.
  2. Only a quarter of those expert computations are active per token under this simplified count.
  3. Attention, routing, communication and shared layers still run, so total cost is not automatically one quarter of a comparable dense model.

When it applies

A large total parameter count does not mean every token accesses all of it. Uneven routing can waste capacity or overload a subset of experts.

Check your understanding

Which counts should accompany an MoE model size claim?

Total parameters, active parameters, routing behaviour and measured end-to-end resource use.
