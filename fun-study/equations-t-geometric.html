Structure-Preserving Long-Time Computation

Can a small short-term error hide a long-term qualitative mistake?

The idea

For oscillatory or conservative systems, long-time questions include phase drift, energy drift and preserved geometry, not just position error at one instant. Structure-preserving methods are designed so that the algorithm does not systematically turn conservative motion into artificial heating or damping.

A symplectic integrator preserves the symplectic structure of its update map. It need not exactly conserve the original Hamiltonian at every step. Modified-energy analysis can explain good long-time behaviour under suitable assumptions, but does not justify arbitrary steps or unlimited integration times.

Read the relationship

q̇=p, ṗ=−q; symplectic Euler: pₙ₊₁=pₙ−hqₙ; qₙ₊₁=qₙ+hpₙ₊₁

q and p are canonical coordinates. The order matters: the position update uses the newly updated momentum.

Work through an example

  1. Start at (q,p)=(1,0) with h=0.1. Symplectic Euler gives p₁=−0.1, q₁=0.99.
  2. Its original energy is 0.49505 rather than exactly 0.5.
  3. Explicit Euler gives (1,−0.1), energy 0.505, and multiplies that energy by 1+h² each step.

When it applies

Symplectic does not mean unconditionally stable, high-order or exactly energy-preserving. Damping, constraints and noncanonical variables require matching numerical formulations.

Check your understanding

Does nonzero energy error disqualify a method from being symplectic?

No. Symplecticity concerns the map's geometry; inspect that property and long-time behaviour separately.
