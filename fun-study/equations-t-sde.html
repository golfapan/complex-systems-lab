Stochastic Differential Equations

Is uncertainty only in the starting point, or added throughout the motion?

The idea

A stochastic differential equation includes continuing random forcing. Even with the same initial state, future paths differ. By contrast, an ensemble of deterministic ODEs with uncertain initial conditions has no new randomness once a starting point has been chosen.

Brownian increments scale like the square root of the time interval. This makes stochastic integration different from ordinary integration of a smooth forcing signal. State the stochastic convention and numerical method: changing from Itô to Stratonovich can alter the drift representation.

Read the relationship

dX=−aXdt+σdW; Xₙ₊₁=(1−aΔt)Xₙ+σ√Δt ξₙ

a>0 is the restoring rate, σ noise strength, and independent ξₙ are standard normal variables. This is the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck model.

Work through an example

  1. Choose a=1, σ=2, Δt=0.01 and Xₙ=1.
  2. If this step draws ξₙ=0.5, the update gives 0.99+2×0.1×0.5=1.09.
  3. A path can move away from zero even though the mean decays; the long-run variance tends to σ²/(2a)=2.

When it applies

Euler–Maruyama is an approximation. Strong path error and weak distributional error are different measures, and the chosen random increments or coupling affect comparisons.

Check your understanding

What goes wrong if √Δt ξ is replaced by Δt ξ?

The accumulated variance per unit time tends to zero as the step shrinks, incorrectly removing the noise.
