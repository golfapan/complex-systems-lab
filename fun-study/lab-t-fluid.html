Dye and vortices in a flow

Why does stirring make thin filaments before mixing makes everything smooth?

Run this experiment →

The idea

Advection carries a field with a velocity, while viscosity smooths vorticity. The model reconstructs an incompressible velocity from vorticity on a periodic two-dimensional domain. Dye makes transport visible, allowing the movement of a pattern to be distinguished from the spreading of that pattern.

Track where dye goes and how its outline stretches. Stretching filaments alone are not a proof of chaotic advection or of a turbulence cascade.

Read the relationship

ωₜ+u·∇ω=νΔω ∇·u=0

ω is scalar vorticity in two dimensions, u is fluid velocity and ν is viscosity. Dye is a separate transported field.

Try this

  1. Compare a vortex pair with a shear layer. Increase viscosity and observe how quickly the visible swirl fades.

When it applies

This is a coarse periodic 2-D teaching solver. Interpolation introduces numerical mixing, and the model is not a full three-dimensional turbulence simulation.
