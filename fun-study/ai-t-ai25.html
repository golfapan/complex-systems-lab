Reinforcement Learning and Policy Optimisation

What changes when your actions affect the data you receive?

The idea

Reinforcement learning evaluates actions through rewards and their delayed consequences. A policy chooses actions from available information. Unlike ordinary supervised learning, a correct action label is often unavailable, and exploration changes which experiences the system gathers.

The learned policy is judged in a closed loop. A reward is a modelling choice, not a complete statement of every real requirement. In robotics, simulation reward, physical constraints, robustness and reliable recovery therefore need separate evaluation.

Read the relationship

J(π)=E[Σₜγᵗrₜ], with 0≤γ<1

π is a policy, r a reward and γ a discount factor. Expected return averages over the randomness and starting conditions specified by the problem.

Work through an example

  1. A robot is rewarded for reaching a target quickly.
  2. It may learn a fast action that exceeds a torque limit or collides with an obstacle if those outcomes are not properly handled.
  3. A higher return then demonstrates success on the written reward, not automatic compliance with every practical requirement.

When it applies

Distribution shift, exploration and imperfect simulators can expose failures absent from training. Compare policies on held-out conditions and report failure rates as well as returns.

Check your understanding

Does the maximum reward from one lucky rollout establish a reliable policy?

No. Reliability requires repeated, appropriately varied trials and explicit checks of constraints.
