Linear PDEs: Equations for Whole Fields

How can a spatial pattern be split into simpler pieces that evolve independently?

The idea

A partial differential equation describes a field whose value depends on position and time. Heat, wave and Poisson equations encode diffusion, propagation and equilibrium respectively. The equation alone is incomplete: the region, boundary data and appropriate initial data define the actual problem.

Separation of variables looks for shapes that retain their spatial form while a time coefficient changes. Boundary conditions select allowed eigenfunctions; a superposition of those modes can match more general initial data. The same spatial modes can decay under heat flow or oscillate under a wave equation, because their time equations differ.

Read the relationship

Heat: ut=κΔu; wave: utt=c²Δu; equilibrium: −Δu=f For u=X(x)T(t): −X″=λX Heat mode: T′=−κλT

κ is diffusivity and c wave speed. Dirichlet data prescribe a boundary value; Neumann data prescribe a normal derivative.

Work through an example

  1. On 0<x<L, hold both endpoint temperatures at zero and start with sin(πx/L).
  2. This spatial shape has eigenvalue λ=(π/L)².
  3. The heat solution is u(x,t)=exp[−κ(π/L)²t]sin(πx/L), keeping its shape while its amplitude decays.

When it applies

A single separated product is not the general solution. Wave problems usually require both initial displacement and initial velocity; boundary compatibility and regularity must be checked.

Check your understanding

Why can an insulated rod approach a nonzero constant temperature?

Insulated ends allow a constant zero-eigenvalue mode. With no sources, total heat is retained and the final constant is the initial spatial average.
