Entanglement and reduced states

Can the whole be known even when each part looks uncertain?

The idea

A composite quantum system uses a tensor-product state space. A pure joint state is entangled if it cannot be written as one state for A times one state for B. A mixed joint state is entangled if it cannot be represented as a probabilistic mixture of product states. To predict measurements on A alone, we trace out B; the result can be mixed even when the joint state is pure.

Entanglement concerns joint structure, not merely two outcomes being correlated. Classical mixtures also produce correlations. Tests involving several measurement choices distinguish the relevant quantum structure. Local randomness and strong joint correlations coexist without allowing an observer to send a chosen message simply by measuring their subsystem.

Read the relationship

|Φ+⟩=(|00⟩+|11⟩)/√2; ρA=TrB(|Φ+⟩⟨Φ+|)=I/2

The partial trace keeps predictions for one subsystem while discarding access to the other. It is not an ordinary coordinate projection of a state vector.

Work through an example

  1. The Bell state's joint purity is Tr(ρAB²)=1.
  2. Each local reduced state has purity Tr((I/2)²)=1/2 and gives zero/one outcomes with equal probabilities.
  3. Joint zero/one results are nevertheless perfectly correlated: both observers obtain the same bit when they compare records.

When it applies

Perfect correlation in just one basis does not establish entanglement. Measurement choices and a correctly modeled joint preparation matter.

Check your understanding

Can one observer choose the distant observer's individual measurement result by selecting a local measurement?

No. Entanglement correlations do not provide controllable faster-than-light signaling through local measurements alone.
