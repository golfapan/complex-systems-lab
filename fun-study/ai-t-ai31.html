Vision Transformers and Multimodal Models

How can an image become a sequence a Transformer can read?

The idea

A Vision Transformer divides an image into patches and projects them into tokens. Multimodal systems connect perceptual encoders, text or other sequences and task-specific objectives. A shared interface does not guarantee that every modality is actually used.

Count and inspect the complete input pathway. Patch size determines sequence length, while positional representation preserves spatial relationships. A model may exploit text-only or dataset shortcuts even when images are supplied, so ablations are an important part of evaluating multimodal claims.

Read the relationship

patch count=(H/P)×(W/P), when P divides both dimensions

H and W are image dimensions and P patch width/height. Extra classification or special tokens increase sequence length beyond the patch count.

Work through an example

  1. A 224×224 image split into 16×16 patches gives 14×14=196 patches.
  2. Adding one classification token produces a sequence of length 197.
  3. Remove the image or scramble image–text pairing and re-evaluate to test whether the claimed visual information contributes.

When it applies

Resizing, patchification and pairing can lose or leak information. A fluent explanation of an image does not by itself establish spatial or causal understanding.

Check your understanding

If image removal leaves performance unchanged, what should you investigate?

Textual shortcuts, weak evaluation tasks and a model that may not be using its visual input.
