Probability and Statistics / 17

Logistic classification, thresholds, and calibration

Why is predicting a probability different from making a decision?

The idea

Logistic regression turns a real-valued score into a probability through the sigmoid function. Its log odds are linear in the model's features, while its output remains between zero and one. Fitting with binary cross-entropy is equivalent to maximizing a Bernoulli likelihood under the model's assumptions.

A decision threshold is a separate choice. It depends on the consequences of false positives and false negatives, not just on the model's preferred probability estimate. Calibration asks whether events assigned probability 0.7 happen about 70% of the time in the relevant population. Accuracy at one threshold cannot answer that question.

Read the relationship

p=1/(1+e⁻ᶻ); classify positive when p>Cfp/(Cfp+Cfn) under the stated two-error cost model

z is the score, Cfp is false-positive cost, and Cfn is false-negative cost. Correct decisions have zero cost in this simplified rule.

Work through an example

  1. Let z=log 3. Then p=1/(1+1/3)=0.75.
  2. With false-positive cost one and false-negative cost four, the threshold is 1/5=0.2.
  3. The decision is positive. Changing costs could change that decision without changing the probability 0.75.

When it applies

For rare positives, predicting negative every time can have high accuracy. Examine precision, recall, calibration, and distribution shift as well.

Check your understanding

Does a classifier with 99% accuracy necessarily detect a class occurring in 1% of cases?

No. It could always predict the majority class and detect none of the positive cases.
