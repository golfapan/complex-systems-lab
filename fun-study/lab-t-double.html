Two coupled pendulums

Why can nearly identical starts lead to visibly different swings?

Run this experiment →

The idea

Each pendulum affects the motion of the other through the shared linkage. Gravity, angle differences and velocity-dependent coupling create nonlinear equations. The demonstration compares a trajectory with a second trajectory started a tiny distance away.

Sensitive dependence concerns a range of motion and initial conditions; not every double-pendulum trajectory is chaotic.

Read the relationship

state=(θ₁,ω₁,θ₂,ω₂) state derivative=f(state)

Angles and angular velocities together form the state. The endpoint path in the picture is only a projection of this four-dimensional state.

Try this

  1. Restart and watch the two pendulums before and after their motions visibly separate. Compare short-term agreement with longer-term behaviour.

When it applies

Finite-distance separation eventually saturates and is not a renormalized Lyapunov calculation. The numerical integrator is not claimed to preserve the exact Hamiltonian indefinitely.
