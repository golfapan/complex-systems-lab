Confidence intervals, standard errors, and bootstrap

What uncertainty does an interval around a sample mean describe?

The idea

A standard error describes how an estimator varies across hypothetical repetitions of the sampling procedure. A confidence interval uses that variation to construct a method with a stated long-run coverage rate. In frequentist inference, the unknown parameter is fixed; the interval changes from sample to sample.

A bootstrap approximates sampling variation by resampling observed data and recalculating the statistic. It can help when a formula is awkward, but resampling must respect the experiment's structure. Repeated measures, clusters, and time dependence require methods that preserve the relevant dependence rather than treating every row as interchangeable.

Read the relationship

x̄±1.96σ/√n; with unknown σ, use x̄±t₀.₉₇₅,ₙ₋₁ s/√n under the appropriate assumptions

σ is population spread; s estimates it. These intervals target the population mean, not the location of a future individual measurement.

Work through an example

  1. Suppose n=100, x̄=50, and the known population standard deviation is 10.
  2. The standard error is 10/√100=1, and the approximate 95% margin is 1.96.
  3. The interval is [48.04,51.96]. Individual observations will usually be much more spread out than this interval.

When it applies

The normal formula requires a suitable sampling distribution; an exact small-sample t interval requires normal errors. Selection bias is not repaired by a narrower interval.

Check your understanding

Does a 95% mean interval contain 95% of the observations?

No. A prediction interval addresses future observations and includes individual-level variability, so it is typically wider.
