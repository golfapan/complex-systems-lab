Relativity / 07

Electric and magnetic fields as one structure

Why can what looks electric in one frame also look magnetic in another?

The idea

Electric and magnetic fields are linked components of the electromagnetic field tensor. Changing inertial frame mixes those components, just as a boost mixes time and space. This structure helps explain why separating electricity and magnetism into unrelated laws would miss an important relativistic connection.

Some combinations of the fields remain invariant under appropriate Lorentz transformations. These give quick checks on whether a frame change could eliminate a field component. They also illustrate a general strategy: before searching through coordinates, identify the quantities that no allowed transformation can change.

Read the relationship

Field invariants include E·B and |E|²−c²|B|²

E and B are measured in one inertial frame. Components and tensor sign conventions must be specified consistently in detailed electromagnetic calculations.

Work through an example

  1. Suppose a region has E·B≠0.
  2. If a frame existed in which E vanished completely, that frame would have E′·B′=0.
  3. This would contradict invariance, so no such frame exists; the same argument prevents eliminating B completely.

When it applies

The invariants constrain possibilities but do not alone provide every transformed component. SI factors of c and tensor conventions must be handled carefully.

Check your understanding

If E·B=0, is that condition alone enough to guarantee a purely electric frame?

No. The other invariant and additional conditions distinguish electric-dominated, magnetic-dominated, and null-field cases.
