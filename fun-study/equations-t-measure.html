Invariant Measures and Statistical Prediction

What can still be predicted when individual trajectories no longer match?

The idea

Instead of asking for one future state, ask how often motion visits a region or what average a sensor records. An invariant measure is a distribution unchanged by the dynamics: individual states keep moving, but their ensemble distribution remains the same.

Ergodicity concerns when long-time averages agree with averages under that measure. It is a property with assumptions, not a reward for running a simulation for many steps. Separate attractors can prevent one trajectory from representing a mixture of possible long-term outcomes.

Read the relationship

μ(Φ⁻ᵗA)=μ(A); time average=(1/T)∫₀ᵀg(x(t))dt; ensemble average=∫g dμ

A is a state-space region, μ assigns probabilities to regions, and g is the observable being averaged.

Work through an example

  1. Consider uniform motion around a circle: θ̇=1, with observable cos θ.
  2. Over complete rotations the time average is zero, matching its average under the uniform circle distribution.
  3. The motion is periodic, not chaotic. Well-defined statistical averages do not require chaos.

When it applies

Finite samples, correlated observations, transients and initial-condition bias affect estimates. A histogram alone is not a proof of ergodicity.

Check your understanding

Can one trajectory approaching one of two stable equilibria represent any mixture of both?

Generally not. Its basin selects one equilibrium; ensemble weights require information about how initial states are distributed.
