Lorentz transformations and invariant intervals

What replaces the Euclidean distance when time is part of the geometry?

The idea

A Lorentz transformation changes spacetime coordinates between inertial frames while preserving the Minkowski interval. It mixes time and the spatial coordinate along relative motion. Unlike an ordinary Euclidean rotation, its preserved quadratic form has opposite signs for time and space, which is crucial to the distinction between causal and spacelike separations.

Choose a sign convention and use it consistently. Here the interval is positive for timelike separation. Individual time and distance components change, but the interval between the same two events does not. Matrix notation connects this preservation rule to linear algebra without making spacetime an ordinary four-dimensional Euclidean space.

Read the relationship

ct′=γ(ct−βx); x′=γ(x−βct); γ=1/√(1−β²); s²=(cΔt)²−Δx²

β=v/c and |β|<1 for the relative velocity of inertial frames. The formulas show a boost along x with aligned origins.

Work through an example

  1. Set c=1, β=0.6, and event separation (Δt,Δx)=(2,1).
  2. Then γ=1.25, giving Δt′=1.75 and Δx′=−0.25.
  3. Both frames obtain s²=3: 4−1=1.75²−0.25². The changed components preserve one spacetime relationship.

When it applies

Signs depend on which frame is moving in which direction. Transform event differences consistently rather than mixing coordinates from separate frames.

Check your understanding

Can a Lorentz boost change a timelike separation into a spacelike one?

No. Preserving the interval preserves its sign and therefore its causal classification.
