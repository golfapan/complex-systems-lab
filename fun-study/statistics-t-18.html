Entropy, cross-entropy, and KL divergence

What does a probability model lose when its probabilities are wrong?

The idea

Entropy measures the average uncertainty of a distribution, or its ideal average coding cost with a chosen logarithm base. Cross-entropy evaluates using another distribution's probabilities to describe those outcomes. Their difference is KL divergence, which measures the extra expected log loss from that mismatch.

The connection to learning is direct: maximizing likelihood minimizes empirical negative log probability. However, a good statistical score answers a specific question about probabilities, not every question about a model. Better log loss does not alone establish fairness, causality, factual reliability, or suitability for a particular decision.

Read the relationship

H(P)=−ΣP log P; H(P,Q)=−ΣP log Q; DKL(P‖Q)=H(P,Q)−H(P)

Base-two logarithms give bits; natural logarithms give nats. If Q assigns zero where P assigns positive mass, KL is infinite.

Work through an example

  1. The true coin distribution is P=(0.5,0.5), with entropy one bit.
  2. A model Q=(0.8,0.2) has cross-entropy −0.5log₂0.8−0.5log₂0.2≈1.32193 bits.
  3. Its KL mismatch is about 0.32193 bit per outcome compared with using the true distribution.

When it applies

KL is generally asymmetric and is not an ordinary distance metric. The support and direction of comparison matter.

Check your understanding

Would swapping P and Q necessarily leave KL unchanged?

No. DKL(P‖Q) and DKL(Q‖P) weight outcomes differently and usually have different values.
