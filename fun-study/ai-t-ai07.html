SGD, Momentum and Adam

Why do different optimisers behave differently on the same loss?

The idea

A mini-batch gradient estimates the gradient of a larger objective. SGD uses that estimate directly, momentum carries an accumulated direction, and Adam maintains estimates of gradient and squared-gradient moments. Learning rate, batch size and scheduling interact with these choices.

Optimisers add dynamics of their own. A second-moment estimate is not the full Hessian, and adaptive scaling is not a universal guarantee of stability. Compare methods with controlled data and initialisation before attributing improvement to the optimiser's name.

Read the relationship

θₜ₊₁=θₜ−ηₜĝₜ; momentum example vₜ=βvₜ₋₁+ĝₜ

ĝ is a sampled gradient, η the learning rate and β a memory coefficient. Adam also uses bias-corrected moment estimates.

Work through an example

  1. For L(w)=w²/2, plain gradient descent becomes w⁺=(1−η)w.
  2. At η=0.5, the magnitude halves; at η=1.5 it halves while alternating sign.
  3. At η=2 it oscillates without shrinking. Stability depends on the update size even for this simple convex objective.

When it applies

An optimiser that works well on one task may not transfer unchanged. Report learning-rate schedules, parameter groups and other experimental conditions.

Check your understanding

Why hold data order and initialisation fixed when comparing optimisers?

Otherwise their effects are mixed with different samples or starting points, obscuring what caused the change.
