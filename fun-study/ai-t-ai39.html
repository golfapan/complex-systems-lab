PEFT, LoRA and Low-Precision Adaptation

Can you adapt a model without training all its weights?

The idea

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning updates a limited part of a model. LoRA freezes a base matrix and learns a low-rank increment. This can greatly reduce trainable parameters while retaining the pretrained transformation.

Low-rank adaptation and low-precision storage are different choices. Combining them still requires memory for optimiser state and activations. Report which layers are adapted, the chosen rank and task quality; fewer trainable parameters do not automatically mean no loss of expressiveness or accuracy.

Read the relationship

W′=W+(α/r)BA; A∈R^(r×din), B∈R^(dout×r)

r is adaptation rank and α a scaling parameter. W is frozen in the basic LoRA setup, while A and B are trained.

Work through an example

  1. A 4096×4096 matrix has 16,777,216 entries.
  2. At rank eight, A and B together have 8×4096+4096×8=65,536 entries.
  3. That ratio concerns this matrix's trainable increment, not every parameter or every byte in the full training job.

When it applies

The useful rank and injection locations depend on the task. Merging the update into weights also depends on deployment and quantisation choices.

Check your understanding

Is a low-rank update the same as replacing the whole pretrained weight by a low-rank matrix?

No. LoRA adds a low-rank change to the retained base matrix.
