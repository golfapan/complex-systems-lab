Randomness and Regularity / 01

Determinism, predictability, and randomness

What exactly are you claiming when you say a system is predictable?

The idea

A deterministic model assigns one future trajectory to a complete initial state and specified inputs, provided its solution is unique. Predictability is a different matter: we may not know that state accurately, may not know the correct model, or may face rapid error growth. A stochastic model instead describes possible paths through a probability law.

Before discussing microscopic and macroscopic behavior, identify the prediction object. Are you predicting one trajectory, a distribution of outcomes, an average, or a noisy measurement? Scale alone does not decide whether the description is deterministic or probabilistic. The same system can support several useful descriptions at different levels of information.

Read the relationship

Trajectory: ẋ=f(x,t); distribution: p(x,t); observation: Y=h(x)+ε

x is a state, p a probability density, h a measurement rule, and ε a measurement-noise term.

Work through an example

  1. A weather forecast says there is a 70% chance of rain tomorrow.
  2. That number can summarize uncertain initial conditions, model limitations, and an ensemble prediction procedure.
  3. It does not, by itself, prove that the underlying atmospheric equations are intrinsically random.

When it applies

Different sources of uncertainty require different evidence. Do not use a single probability statement to settle a philosophical interpretation.

Check your understanding

Can a deterministic equation produce an uncertain forecast?

Yes. Unknown initial conditions and sensitive dependence can make trajectory forecasts uncertain even when the chosen evolution rule is deterministic.
