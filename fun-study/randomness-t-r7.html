Randomness and Regularity / 07

Entropy, coarse-graining, and the arrow of time

How can reversible microscopic rules coexist with an apparent approach to equilibrium?

The idea

A macro-description groups many microscopic states into a smaller set of distinguishable conditions. Some macroconditions correspond to vastly more microscopic arrangements than others. Under appropriate dynamical and preparation assumptions, systems are therefore likely to spend much of their time in the larger regions of state space.

Entropy depends on the description being used. Fine-grained Gibbs entropy is preserved by suitable Hamiltonian evolution, while coarse-graining discards detailed structures that develop in the density. These statements are compatible. They do not mean that every trajectory always increases every entropy, or that a probability argument alone explains the special low-entropy preparation of a system.

Read the relationship

SB=kB log Ω; SG=−kB∫ρ logρ dΓ

Ω counts compatible microstates; ρ is a phase-space density. Coarse-grained and fine-grained quantities must be distinguished.

Work through an example

  1. Place four distinguishable particles independently into the two halves of a box, with equal probabilities.
  2. A two-and-two split has C(4,2)=6 arrangements, while all four on the left has one.
  3. Larger systems concentrate more sharply near balanced splits, but small systems can still display substantial fluctuations.

When it applies

Equilibrium counting needs a justified ensemble and dynamical assumptions. Coarse-graining is a loss of descriptive information, not a new microscopic force.

Check your understanding

Must an isolated Hamiltonian system's fine-grained density become a featureless constant?

No. It can stretch into fine structure while preserving the appropriate fine-grained entropy.
