Avalanches in a sandpile

How can adding one grain trigger a chain of local rearrangements?

Run this experiment →

The idea

A site with at least four grains topples, sending one grain to each of its four neighbours. Those neighbours may then topple. Slow additions and repeated local redistribution create avalanches with different sizes, while grains can leave at the open boundary.

Keep the accounting straight: grains added equal grains stored plus grains lost, relative to the starting configuration.

Read the relationship

hᵢ≥4 ⇒ hᵢ←hᵢ−4 each nearest neighbour receives one grain

Avalanche size here counts toppling events. It is not necessarily the number of distinct affected sites.

Try this

  1. Add grains by stepping. Compare many small events with occasional larger cascades and inspect the accumulated size histogram.

When it applies

A finite avalanche histogram is not proof of a power law or universal self-organized criticality. Boundary loss and the drive protocol are essential parts of this model.
