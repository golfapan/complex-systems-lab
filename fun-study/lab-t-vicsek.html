Collective motion with noise

Can local agreement overcome random errors in heading?

Run this experiment →

The idea

Each moving agent averages nearby direction vectors, adds angular noise, and travels at a fixed speed. The competition between alignment and noise can change the group's overall heading agreement even though each agent sees only a small neighbourhood.

Near-unity polarization means agents point similarly. It does not imply that their positions form one compact cluster.

Read the relationship

θᵢ⁺=arg Σⱼ∈Nᵢ exp(iθⱼ)+noise xᵢ⁺=xᵢ+v₀e(θᵢ⁺)

arg extracts the angle of the summed unit vectors. v₀ is a fixed step speed, and the angular noise distribution is part of the rule.

Try this

  1. Raise noise, reset, and compare the polarization trace with a low-noise run. Allow a transient before comparing averages.

When it applies

This finite periodic system does not establish an infinite-system phase transition or a universal critical threshold. It is also not the full three-rule Boids model.
