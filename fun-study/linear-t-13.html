Low-Rank Approximation and PCA

Can many features be described by just a few important directions?

The idea

Truncated SVD keeps the strongest singular components of a matrix, giving the best rank-limited reconstruction in the Frobenius norm. For a centred sample-by-feature data matrix, this becomes principal component analysis: find orthogonal directions capturing the greatest sample variance.

Compression, explanation and prediction are different goals. A direction with large variance can be irrelevant to a target, while a small-variance feature can be informative. Centring removes the mean; standardising also changes feature scales. Both decisions affect what geometry the method sees, and fitting them on test data leaks information.

Read the relationship

Ak=Σᵢ₌₁ᵏσᵢui viᵀ ‖A−Ak‖F²=Σᵢ>kσᵢ² C=XcᵀXc/(N−1)

Rows of Xc are centred samples and columns are features. Its right singular vectors give PCA feature directions; N>1 is needed for this sample covariance.

Work through an example

  1. A two-feature sample has covariance C=[[5,4],[4,5]].
  2. Its eigenvalues are 9 and 1, with first direction (1,1)/√2.
  3. The first component explains 9/(9+1)=90% of the sample variance, leaving 10% in the orthogonal direction.

When it applies

The optimality claim concerns a specified reconstruction norm and rank constraint. It is not a universal claim about useful features, causal mechanisms or future dynamics.

Check your understanding

Does retaining 99% of variance prove a reduced model will predict accurately?

No. Reconstruction and prediction measure different things. Test the actual task and new data rather than inferring performance from variance alone.
