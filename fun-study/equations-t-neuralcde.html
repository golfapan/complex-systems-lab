Neural CDEs and Observation-Driven Sequences

How can a hidden state respond when new measurements arrive?

The idea

A basic initial-value Neural ODE follows a trajectory determined by its initial state and vector field. A Neural CDE interpolates observations into an input path and learns how increments of that path drive its hidden state, allowing later measurements to influence the evolution.

Interpolation, time channels and missingness indicators are modelling choices. They decide what information is available and how it arrives. Continuous interpolation does not reveal unobserved physical events; predictive applications must also avoid using future measurements to construct inputs available only in hindsight.

Read the relationship

z(t)=z₀+∫₀ᵗfθ(z(s))dX(s); example X=(t,x), dz=−zdt+2dx

Each column of fθ responds to an input channel. Including time allows internal evolution even when the measured signal is temporarily constant.

Work through an example

  1. On one interpolation segment, take x(t)=3t and z₀=0.
  2. The hidden equation is ż=−z+6, giving z(t)=6(1−e⁻ᵗ).
  3. For a constant measured signal, dx=0 and the same model instead follows ż=−z.

When it applies

CDEs, jump models, SDEs and recurrent updates inject information differently. A path representation and its assumptions are part of the model, not neutral preprocessing.

Check your understanding

Is placing all observations into z₀ the same as driving a CDE through time?

No. Initial encoding and ongoing path-driven updates use observation timing in different ways.
