Neural Operators: FNO and DeepONet

Can one model learn an entire family of solution problems?

The idea

A neural operator learns a mapping from an input function to an output function, such as an initial temperature profile to the temperature one second later. Training must vary the input conditions; fitting one solution does not establish a cross-problem operator.

A Fourier Neural Operator parameterises spatial mixing in frequency space. DeepONet combines a branch network encoding sampled input functions with a trunk network encoding output locations. Their representations differ, even though both address function-to-function learning. Resolution, geometry and boundary handling remain substantive choices.

Read the relationship

G:a(x)↦u(x); FNO sketch: v⁺=σ(Wv+F⁻¹[R·F(v)]); DeepONet: G(a)(x)≈Σbₖ(a)tₖ(x)

F denotes a Fourier transform, R learned frequency weights, and b and t the branch and trunk outputs. These are structural sketches, not complete implementations.

Work through an example

  1. For zero-boundary heat flow, restrict initial data to a(x)=A sin x and fix output time one.
  2. The exact map sends it to Ae⁻¹sin x; A=2 gives amplitude approximately 0.73576.
  3. Learning only A=1 does not demonstrate generalisation to other frequencies, shapes or boundaries.

When it applies

Function-space language does not guarantee arbitrary-resolution or out-of-distribution accuracy. Sampling, truncation and training coverage constrain what can be predicted.

Check your understanding

Is a network receiving only coordinates and fitting one fixed solution automatically an operator learner?

No. It must also receive varying problem conditions and learn the mapping across instances.
