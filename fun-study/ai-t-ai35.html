GPUs, Precision and Distributed Training

Why does a FLOP count not tell you the elapsed training time?

The idea

Training also moves weights, activations and gradients through memory and communicates between devices. Poor utilisation or communication can dominate arithmetic. Profiling identifies the bottleneck before choosing a parallelisation or precision strategy.

Data parallelism splits examples, tensor parallelism splits tensor operations, and pipeline parallelism splits stages. Mixed precision and activation recomputation trade memory, speed and numerical behaviour. They are different choices whose benefits depend on the hardware and workload.

Read the relationship

Observed throughput=useful processed tokens / elapsed wall-clock time

Specify whether the numerator includes padding, repeated work or only useful tokens. End-to-end measurements should include the relevant overheads.

Work through an example

  1. Storing one million weights in two-byte format needs about two million bytes for those weights alone.
  2. Training also needs gradients, optimiser state, activations and temporary buffers.
  3. A setup fitting the weight array may therefore still run out of memory during backpropagation.

When it applies

Lower precision can overflow or lose important detail. A faster kernel does not guarantee a faster complete training job or equivalent quality.

Check your understanding

Why measure before changing a distributed strategy?

Different jobs are limited by compute, memory bandwidth or communication; the same change can help one and hinder another.
