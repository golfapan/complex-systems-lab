Gray–Scott patterns

How can local reactions and spreading generate spots that split or fade?

Run this experiment →

The idea

One field feeds a reaction that produces another, while supply and removal keep the system away from a closed equilibrium. Diffusion couples neighbouring locations. Small changes in feed or removal rates can change which structures persist.

A pattern is an outcome of reaction, diffusion, forcing and initial conditions together. Similar pictures do not prove identical mechanisms.

Read the relationship

uₜ=DᵤΔu−uv²+F(1−u) vₜ=DᵥΔv+uv²−(F+k)v

F is feed rate, k is removal rate and uv² is a nonlinear reaction term. The two fields spread at different rates.

Try this

  1. Change the feed rate slightly, restart with the same seed, and wait long enough to compare splitting, stable spots and collapse.

When it applies

The simulation is dimensionless and coarse. Its parameters are not calibrated chemical measurements, and visible pattern formation alone is not a linear-instability proof.
