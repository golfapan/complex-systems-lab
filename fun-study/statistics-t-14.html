Model checks, ANOVA, and generalized linear models

How do you know whether a regression model is answering the right question?

The idea

A fitted model needs diagnostic checks. Residual patterns may reveal missing curvature, changing variance, dependence, or unusually influential observations. These are reasons to reconsider the model, not automatic instructions to delete awkward data. The required distributional assumptions usually concern conditional errors, rather than every raw input being normally distributed.

ANOVA compares structured mean models and can be expressed within linear regression. Generalized linear models extend the response distribution and connect its mean to a linear predictor through a link function. For example, a log link keeps a count mean positive, while a logistic link constrains probabilities to the unit interval.

Read the relationship

R²=1−SSE/SST; F=[(SSErestricted−SSEfull)/q]/[SSEfull/(n−d)]

SSE is residual squared error, SST is total squared variation, q is the added parameter count, and d is the full model's parameter count.

Work through an example

  1. Suppose SST=100, restricted SSE=60, and full SSE=40, with n=30, d=3, and q=1.
  2. The full model has R²=1−40/100=0.6.
  3. Its nested-model statistic is F=20/(40/27)=13.5; interpretation requires the relevant F distribution and valid model assumptions.

When it applies

A transformed response changes the target: reversing a nonlinear transformation of a fitted mean does not generally recover the original-scale mean.

Check your understanding

Does a Poisson regression automatically allow variance much larger than its mean?

No. The basic Poisson model equates conditional mean and variance; overdispersion calls for further investigation or another model.
