Eigenvalues, Eigenvectors and Evolution

Which directions keep their identity when a matrix acts repeatedly?

The idea

An eigenvector is a nonzero direction that a matrix maps onto its own line. The associated eigenvalue tells us the signed or complex multiplier. If enough independent eigenvectors form a basis, changing into that basis separates the action into independent modes.

The time interpretation matters. Repeated discrete updates multiply each mode by λ at every step, so magnitudes below one decay. A continuous equation x′=Ax instead evolves each mode through eˡᵗ, so real parts below zero decay. A matrix suitable for one stability statement is not automatically suitable for the other.

Read the relationship

Av=λv, v≠0 If diagonalizable: A=VΛV⁻¹ Discrete: xk=Aᵏx₀; continuous: x(t)=e^(At)x₀

V contains independent eigenvectors; Λ contains eigenvalues. The diagonalisation formula requires enough independent vectors, not merely a list of roots.

Work through an example

  1. For A=diag(0.5,2) and x₀=(1,1), the axes are eigenvectors.
  2. After k discrete updates, xk=(0.5ᵏ,2ᵏ).
  3. One component decays while the other grows, so the overall discrete system is unstable.

When it applies

Eigenvalues on a stability boundary require attention to Jordan structure. A repeated eigenvalue does not automatically mean a matrix is defective; some repeated-root matrices have a full eigenbasis.

Check your understanding

What happens if the same A=diag(0.5,2) is used in x′=Ax?

Both continuous modes grow, because both eigenvalues have positive real parts. Discrete and continuous stability criteria differ.
