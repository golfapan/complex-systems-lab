Sensitivities, Adjoint Equations and Autodiff

How can you find which parameters change a final result?

The idea

A sensitivity is a derivative of a result with respect to a parameter, input or initial state. Forward sensitivities travel with the state. When there are many parameters but one scalar objective, an adjoint can efficiently carry the objective's influence backwards along a trajectory.

Automatic differentiation applies the chain rule to a numerical program. A continuous adjoint is derived before discretisation; reverse differentiation of a solver follows its actual discrete steps. These routes can differ at finite tolerance, especially with adaptivity, interpolation, stiffness or events.

Read the relationship

ẋ=f(x,θ,t); L=ℓ(x(T)); ȧ=−(∂f/∂x)ᵀa; a(T)=∇ℓ; dL/dθ=∫aᵀfθ dt

The displayed gradient assumes a parameter-independent initial state and only a terminal cost. Other dependencies require additional terms.

Work through an example

  1. Take ẋ=θx, x(0)=1, T=1 and L=x(1)=e^θ.
  2. The adjoint is a(t)=e^[θ(1−t)] and fθ=x(t)=e^(θt).
  3. Their product is e^θ, so integration over one second gives dL/dθ=e^θ, matching direct calculus.

When it applies

A correct gradient does not make optimisation easy. Reverse reconstruction can be numerically unreliable, and checkpoints or specialised sensitivity methods may be required.

Check your understanding

Does autodiff necessarily return the exact derivative of the ideal continuous model?

It normally differentiates the executed computation, which still contains numerical approximations.
