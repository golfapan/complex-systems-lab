Score-Based Diffusion and Probability Flow

How can learning to describe noise help generate data?

The idea

A score-based model specifies a forward process that gradually perturbs data towards an easy distribution. It learns the score at different noise levels, then uses that information in a reverse stochastic process or a related probability-flow ODE to generate samples.

The score is a gradient of log density, not a class label or one uniquely correct clean picture. It indicates how density changes near the current location at a particular time. Training the score and choosing a numerical sampling procedure are separate design decisions.

Read the relationship

sθ(x,t)≈∇log pₜ(x); dX=fdt+g(t)dW; probability flow: Ẋ=f−(g²/2)sθ

This displayed flow formula assumes spatially constant scalar noise g(t). Generative integration normally runs from the noise time towards the data time.

Work through an example

  1. For pₜ=N(0,s²), the score is −x/s².
  2. At s²=4 and x=2, the score is −0.5, pointing towards higher density near the centre.
  3. If f=0 and g=1, forward probability-flow velocity is +0.25 there; integrating backwards reverses the corresponding spreading.

When it applies

Equivalent time marginals do not mean identical random paths. Score error, time truncation and solver error affect samples; general diffusion matrices need the appropriate formula.

Check your understanding

Is a score at one noise level enough to define the full generation process?

Usually not. The process needs a time-dependent score, noise schedule, starting distribution and consistent integration direction.
