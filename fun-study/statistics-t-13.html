Linear regression: fitting a line and interpreting a model

What line best summarizes a relationship, and what does that line promise?

The idea

Least squares chooses coefficients that minimize the sum of squared residuals. A linear regression model is linear in its coefficients, so it may still contain curved features such as x². The fitted line summarizes an assumed conditional mean relationship; it does not turn an observed association into a causal law.

The matrix viewpoint connects regression to projection. Predictions lie in the column space of the design matrix, and the residual is orthogonal to that space at an ordinary least-squares solution. Correlated or redundant columns make coefficient estimates unstable or nonunique even when predictions can still be calculated.

Read the relationship

yᵢ=β₀+β₁xᵢ+εᵢ; β̂₁=Σ(xᵢ−x̄)(yᵢ−ȳ)/Σ(xᵢ−x̄)²

β₀ is the intercept, β₁ is the slope, and ε represents departures from the conditional mean model.

Work through an example

  1. Fit x=(0,1,2) and y=(1,2,2). Their means are one and 5/3.
  2. The centered cross-product sum is one and the x squared-deviation sum is two, so the slope is 1/2.
  3. The intercept is 5/3−1/2=7/6, giving ŷ=7/6+x/2.

When it applies

A high training R² is not evidence of generalization or causation. Extrapolating outside the observed range requires additional justification.

Check your understanding

Does a model β₀+β₁x+β₂x² count as linear regression?

Yes: it is linear in the unknown coefficients. The response can still curve as a function of x.
