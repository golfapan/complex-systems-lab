Vectors, Space and Parametric Motion

Can speed stay constant while acceleration is nonzero?

The idea

Vectors preserve direction as well as magnitude. A dot product measures alignment and projection; a three-dimensional cross product measures oriented area and supplies a perpendicular direction. They are different operations with different kinds of outputs, so they cannot be swapped simply because both resemble multiplication.

A parametric curve records position as a parameter changes. When that parameter is time, its first derivative is velocity and its second derivative is acceleration. The path's shape and the rate at which it is traversed are separate: walking the same circle faster changes the velocity without changing the circle.

Read the relationship

v=r′(t), a=r″(t), speed=|v| a·b=|a||b|cosθ |a×b|=|a||b|sinθ

r is a position vector. Speed is a scalar; velocity is a vector. The direction of a×b follows the right-hand rule.

Work through an example

  1. Take r(t)=(R cosωt,R sinωt), with R,ω>0.
  2. Differentiation gives constant speed Rω.
  3. But a=−ω²r points towards the centre, with magnitude Rω²=v²/R.

When it applies

A regular curve has nonzero tangent velocity. Projection onto b requires b≠0; changing orientation can change the sign of an oriented area or flux.

Check your understanding

Why is constant speed not the same as zero acceleration?

Acceleration measures changes in the velocity vector, including direction. Circular motion changes direction continuously even when its speed is fixed.
