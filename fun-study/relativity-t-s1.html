Relativity / 01

Events, reference frames, and worldlines

What must two observers agree to compare before discussing time?

The idea

An event is an occurrence assigned a time and place, such as a flash at one detector. A reference frame supplies coordinates for events using its clock and ruler conventions. Different inertial frames may assign different coordinates to the same event without disagreeing about which occurrence is being described.

A worldline is a sequence of events associated with an object's history. Comparing frames means transforming the coordinates of those same events, not choosing a different pair because their descriptions sound similar. Special relativity begins with inertial frames and the same vacuum light speed c, then asks which relationships between events survive coordinate changes.

Read the relationship

X=(ct,x,y,z); a worldline is X(λ)

ct puts the time coordinate in length units. λ is a parameter along a worldline, often proper time for a massive object.

Work through an example

  1. A lamp flashes at one location and a detector records the arrival elsewhere.
  2. These are two events. A moving observer gives each a new time and position.
  3. The transformed time difference must be computed between those same emission and reception events, not between an unrelated pair of clock readings.

When it applies

Coordinates are not identical to what a camera sees: light-travel delay affects an image and must be modeled separately.

Check your understanding

Is a flash a worldline or an event?

One specified flash occurrence is an event. A continuously operating lamp traces a worldline through many events.
