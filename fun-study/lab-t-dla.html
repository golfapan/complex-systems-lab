Complex Systems Lab / 18

Diffusion-limited aggregation

Why do randomly wandering particles build branches instead of a smooth ball?

Run this experiment →

The idea

A particle performs a random walk until it touches the existing cluster and sticks. Exposed tips intercept arriving walkers more easily than sheltered interior gaps. This growth bias emerges from geometry and access, without a particle explicitly computing an attractive force.

The particle rule is simple, but the evolving cluster changes the probability of future attachment. History is stored in the geometry.

Read the relationship

walker → random step touch cluster → attach permanently

The state includes the occupied lattice and the current walker. The launch and discard regions are finite simulation choices.

Try this

  1. Watch where new particles attach. Compare how often a protruding tip grows with how often a narrow inlet fills.

When it applies

The lattice, finite domain and walker-launch rule affect the picture. The displayed cluster is not a precise measurement of an asymptotic fractal dimension.
