Pretraining Data and Deduplication

What does a language-model data pipeline actually change?

The idea

A data pipeline records sources, filters documents, removes duplicates, builds splits, tokenises and mixes examples. These operations define the training distribution. More tokens do not automatically compensate for duplicated, irrelevant or poorly labelled material.

Deduplication is also an evaluation issue: near-copies shared between training and test can inflate scores. Keep provenance, document-level grouping and inspectable samples of filtering decisions. Data quality is not one universal number; it depends on the intended language, task and coverage.

Read the relationship

sources → cleaning → deduplication → splitting → tokenisation → batching

Each arrow can change which examples remain and their sampling weights. A tokenizer fitted or chosen for a corpus is another part of the pipeline.

Work through an example

  1. Save one article under ten different filenames.
  2. The resulting files are not ten independent sources of evidence or ten diverse training examples.
  3. If a near-copy also appears in the test set, apparent performance can reward recall of overlap rather than the intended generalisation.

When it applies

Exact hashes miss paraphrases and near-duplicates, while aggressive filtering can remove useful minority content. Audit both retained and rejected samples.

Check your understanding

Why split by document rather than randomly by nearby text chunks?

Neighbouring chunks share content and context; separating them can create misleadingly easy evaluation.
