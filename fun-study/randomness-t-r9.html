Randomness and Regularity / 09

Quantum probabilities and questions about reality

What can an experiment establish without settling every interpretation?

The idea

Quantum mechanics gives operational rules connecting a prepared state and a specified measurement to probabilities of outcomes. For an eigenstate of the measured observable, an outcome can be certain; for a general superposition, the same measurement usually has several possible results. The state between measurements can still evolve deterministically.

Questions about whether randomness is fundamental require care about interpretation and assumptions. A useful first step is to separate what a model predicts from an extra story about what exists underneath it. Experimental probability statements should identify the preparation and observable, rather than treating all microscopic measurements as one undifferentiated kind of randomness.

Read the relationship

Prepared state + specified measurement → a testable outcome distribution

The observable and state jointly determine probabilities. An uncertainty relation concerns spreads within a state, not a ban on individual readings.

Work through an example

  1. An electron position measurement can produce a definite recorded position.
  2. Repeating an identical preparation gives a distribution of such records, not necessarily the same position each time.
  3. The restriction on simultaneously narrowing position and momentum distributions is a different claim from saying an electron cannot be measured.

When it applies

Interpretations may explain the formalism differently. Do not claim an elementary probability example rules out every possible hidden-state theory.

Check your understanding

Does deterministic Schrödinger evolution imply every measurement outcome is predetermined within the usual operational rules?

No. It fixes state evolution, while the Born rule supplies measurement probabilities.
