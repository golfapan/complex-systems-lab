Training, validation, testing, and data leakage

Which decisions are allowed to use which observations?

The idea

Training data fit a model. Validation data help choose features, hyperparameters, and procedures. Test data evaluate the final frozen procedure. Looking repeatedly at test performance and changing the model turns that test set into another source of training information, even if its labels never enter the optimizer directly.

Preprocessing belongs inside the training process. Scaling, imputation, feature selection, and dimensionality reduction must be learned on the appropriate training fold, then applied unchanged to its validation fold. The split should also match the intended use: people, machines, and time periods may need to stay together to prevent leakage.

Read the relationship

MSE=Σ(yᵢ−ŷᵢ)²/n; CV=ΣₖnₖMSEₖ/Σₖnₖ

MSE measures squared prediction error; nₖ is a validation fold's size. Weight folds correctly when their sizes differ.

Work through an example

  1. For three equal-sized validation folds, model A has MSE values 4, 6, and 5.
  2. Its cross-validation average is five; model B's values 3, 8, and 7 average six.
  3. Choose A by this criterion, freeze the full procedure, and assess it once on independent test data.

When it applies

Random splitting can be misleading for time series or repeated measurements. Nested cross-validation separates model selection from performance estimation when data are scarce.

Check your understanding

Can you standardize all data before cross-validation because labels are not used?

Usually not. The validation distribution would influence training transformations, leaking information into the evaluated pipeline.
