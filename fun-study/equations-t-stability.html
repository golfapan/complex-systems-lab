Stability and Basins of Attraction

After a disturbance, does a system stay near its target or return to it?

The idea

Lyapunov stability means sufficiently small initial errors remain small. Asymptotic stability additionally requires convergence back to the target. The basin of attraction contains the initial states that eventually reach that target; it can be much smaller than the whole state space.

A Lyapunov function acts like a mathematical height. It is smallest at the target, and its change along motion helps establish what the system can do. It need not be physical energy. Its positivity, derivative and region of validity must match the theorem being used.

Read the relationship

V̇=∇V·f; ẋ=−x+x³; V=x²/2 gives V̇=−x²(1−x²)

V̇ is the derivative along a solution. Local linearisation and a global basin are different levels of information.

Work through an example

  1. The scalar example has equilibria at −1, 0 and 1.
  2. Inside −1<x<1, V decreases away from zero and trajectories approach zero.
  3. At x=1.2, the rate is −1.2+1.728=0.528, pointing outwards. The basin of zero is only (−1,1).

When it applies

A nonincreasing V does not alone prove asymptotic convergence; invariant-set arguments may be needed. Zero-real-part linearised eigenvalues also require further analysis.

Check your understanding

Is the origin of an ideal undamped spring asymptotically stable?

No. Nearby trajectories remain on closed energy curves rather than converging to the origin.
