Computation Graphs and Backpropagation

How does a final error tell an early weight which way to move?

The idea

The forward pass builds intermediate values. Backpropagation starts from a scalar loss and applies the chain rule backwards, combining all downstream contributions at shared nodes. It computes derivatives; an optimiser separately uses those derivatives to change parameters.

A computation graph makes dependencies explicit. If one variable influences the loss along two routes, both contributions must be added. Keeping track of whether a derivative concerns an input, an activation or a weight prevents many plausible-looking but incorrect updates.

Read the relationship

δₗ=(Wₗ₊₁ᵀδₗ₊₁)⊙σ′(zₗ); ∂L/∂Wₗ=δₗaₗ₋₁ᵀ

The layer formula uses single-example column vectors. δ is the derivative with respect to a layer's preactivation, and ⊙ is elementwise multiplication.

Work through an example

  1. Let x=2, w=0.5, b=0 and target y=2. The linear prediction is one.
  2. For L=(ŷ−y)²/2, gradients are ∂L/∂w=−2 and ∂L/∂b=−1.
  3. A step of 0.1 gives w=0.7 and b=0.1; the new prediction is 1.5 and loss 0.125.

When it applies

A decreasing loss on one step is useful evidence, but does not verify every graph branch, derivative or batch reduction.

Check your understanding

Is backpropagation itself the rule that chooses the learning rate?

No. Backpropagation computes gradients; the optimiser and schedule define how they are used.
