Interpretability, Ablation and Causal Evidence

What can an attractive heatmap really establish?

The idea

Attributions, probes and visualisations suggest where to investigate. They do not automatically explain the mechanism behind a model's decision. Attention weights are one internal calculation, not a complete causal account of the output.

Controlled changes test stronger claims. Remove a modality, replace an input feature or alter one module while holding other factors as constant as possible. A performance change is still conditional on the intervention and test set; seek alternative explanations rather than equating one ablation with definitive proof.

Read the relationship

Intervention comparison=original performance−performance after a controlled change

An ablation removes or changes a component. A causal claim requires attention to confounders, distribution shifts introduced by the intervention and the intended comparison.

Work through an example

  1. A visual question-answering system performs equally well after images are removed.
  2. It may be using textual shortcuts, but the test may also be too easy to require images.
  3. Design a matched set of genuinely image-dependent questions to distinguish those explanations.

When it applies

Removing a component can itself create unfamiliar inputs. Interpretability findings should state the conditions tested and the alternatives not ruled out.

Check your understanding

Does a bright attention region prove a feature caused the final decision?

No. It is a clue; targeted interventions and suitable controls are needed for stronger evidence.
