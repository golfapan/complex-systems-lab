Encoders, Decoders and BERT/GPT-Style Models

How can similar attention machinery support different tasks?

The idea

An encoder often uses bidirectional context to build input representations. A causal decoder reads only the prefix when predicting a next token. An encoder–decoder system additionally uses cross-attention to condition generated outputs on encoded inputs.

Training objectives determine what information is permitted and which positions contribute loss. BERT-style masking and GPT-style autoregression are representative routes, not names for every possible architecture. Discuss visibility and prediction targets before comparing labels.

Read the relationship

Masked-token prediction uses permitted context; autoregression uses p(xₜ|x₁,…,xₜ₋₁)

A prefix contains earlier positions only. Cross-attention is different from allowing a causal output stream to see its unknown future targets.

Work through an example

  1. To fill a missing middle word in a supplied sentence, both left and right context may be available.
  2. To generate a new final word, only the existing prefix is known.
  3. The same apparent word prediction can therefore have different available evidence and a different training loss.

When it applies

Bidirectional scoring is not automatically a deployable left-to-right generator. Generation procedures must match the model's training and visibility assumptions.

Check your understanding

Could a next-token test fairly let the model read the correct future continuation?

No. That would leak the information it is supposed to predict.
