LoRA: Learning a Low-Rank Update

Can a large weight matrix adapt without training every entry?

The idea

Low-rank structure can restrict what is learned, not just compress something already known. LoRA keeps a base matrix fixed and learns its change as a product of two thinner matrices. The model can therefore adapt through a smaller set of trainable parameters.

This is different from taking an SVD of a completed update and truncating it afterwards. LoRA imposes the factorised parameterisation during training. The rank determines an allowed family of updates, while scaling controls their contribution. Whether that family is sufficient depends on the task and the modules being adapted.

Read the relationship

W=W₀+(α/r)BA B∈ℝ^(m×r), A∈ℝ^(r×n), rank(BA)≤r Factor parameters: r(m+n), versus mn

W₀ is frozen, r is the chosen update rank and α the scaling parameter in this common convention. A and B here are adapter factors.

Work through an example

  1. A full 1024×1024 matrix contains 1,048,576 weights.
  2. With r=8, the two LoRA factors contain 8(1024+1024)=16,384 trainable weights.
  3. That is 1/64 of this layer's full weight count; it does not imply the same ratio for total memory or training time.

When it applies

Fewer trainable weights do not guarantee equal performance or eliminate the need for representative training data. Other trainable modules and optimiser states affect actual resources.

Check your understanding

Is a rank-8 LoRA update required to have rank exactly eight?

No. The product has rank at most eight and can be lower if the learned factors are themselves rank-deficient.
