Reinforcement Learning, MPC and Control

How are learning a policy and repeatedly planning actions related?

The idea

Actions change later observations and outcomes, so immediate reward is not the whole objective. Reinforcement learning uses interaction or recorded experience to learn a policy, value function or model. Model predictive control uses a model to optimise a finite future plan, executes its first action and replans after observing again.

These approaches can be combined, but accurate prediction, high reward and safe stable feedback are different outcomes. An objective can reward a shortcut that violates an unstated requirement. Evaluation must follow the actual closed-loop task and disturbances, rather than only attractive nominal trajectories.

Read the relationship

V(s)=maxₐ E[r(s,a,s′)+γV(s′)]; MPC executes the first action of each newly optimised horizon

s is state, a action and 0≤γ<1 a discount factor. Reward maximisation corresponds to cost minimisation with a sign change.

Work through an example

  1. Let x=1, u∈{−1,0,1}, x⁺=x+u and one-step cost (x+u)²+0.1u².
  2. The costs are 0.1, 1 and 4.1, so this calculation selects u=−1.
  3. MPC would repeat from the next measured state; a learned policy could approximate this decision rule. The toy calculation is not a complete RL algorithm.

When it applies

Recursive feasibility and stability require further conditions. Model error, exploration, dataset coverage and delays can change the deployed result.

Check your understanding

Does a small penalty on constraint violation guarantee no violations?

No. A soft objective may trade a violation for reward; a guarantee requires appropriate constraints and assumptions.
