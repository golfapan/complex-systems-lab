A wake behind a cylinder

How can a steady inlet produce an alternating downstream wake?

Run this experiment →

The idea

Flow around an obstacle couples transport, viscous effects and boundary conditions. The demonstration uses a D2Q9 lattice-Boltzmann scheme with an inlet, an outlet and a solid cylinder. Vorticity makes alternating rotation in the wake visible.

A moving wake is a time-dependent field, not simply dye emitted from the cylinder. Resolution and channel walls can affect its behaviour.

Read the relationship

Re=U D/ν lattice populations: collision + streaming

U is a specified inlet velocity scale, D the cylinder diameter and ν the kinematic viscosity. A peak-based Reynolds number differs from a mean-based one.

Try this

  1. Watch startup before judging the wake. Compare downstream vorticity with a sampled transverse-velocity trace.

When it applies

This coarse finite-channel solver is not an engineering benchmark. It does not verify a universal onset Reynolds number or a universal Strouhal value.
