Differential Equations, Dynamics and AI / 05

Hybrid, Constrained and Switching Systems

What happens when smooth motion meets a sudden event?

The idea

A bouncing ball follows a differential equation in the air, then changes velocity abruptly at impact. A hybrid model specifies each continuous mode, the event that triggers a switch, and a reset rule for the state. A smaller ordinary time step does not replace a missing impact law.

Constraints are a related but distinct issue. A rigid rod restricts which positions and velocities are admissible, and may lead to differential-algebraic equations. Initial conditions must satisfy those restrictions. Smooth evolution, event resets and algebraic constraints therefore need different pieces of mathematical description.

Read the relationship

In flight: q̇=v, v̇=−g; impact at q=0 with v<0; v⁺=−ev⁻

Superscripts − and + indicate immediately before and after impact. The restitution coefficient e lies between zero and one in this ideal model.

Work through an example

  1. Suppose the incoming velocity is v⁻=−4 m/s and e=0.8.
  2. The outgoing velocity is +3.2 m/s. Unit-mass kinetic energy changes from 8 to 5.12 joules.
  3. The retained fraction is e²=0.64; restart the flight equation from the reset state.

When it applies

Event timing errors, friction, simultaneous contacts and infinitely accumulating impacts complicate simulation. Gradients through an event must also account for the changed event time.

Check your understanding

Does setting e=1 guarantee an exactly energy-conserving numerical simulation?

No. The ideal impact law preserves the impact energy, but time integration and event localisation may still introduce error.
