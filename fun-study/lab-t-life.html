Complex Systems Lab / 02

Conway's Game of Life

How can something travel when the cells themselves never move?

Run this experiment →

The idea

Each grid cell is either alive or dead. Every cell reads the previous generation's eight neighbours, then all cells update together. A glider moves as a pattern because cells appear at its leading edge and disappear behind it. There is no separate instruction to move the glider.

A repeating shape may oscillate, translate, or remain still. These are different behaviours produced by the same local rule.

Read the relationship

A cell is alive next generation if n=3, or if it is alive now and n=2.

n is the number of live neighbours. The standard rule is written B3/S23: birth at three, survival at two or three.

Try this

  1. Pause the glider and advance four generations by hand. Track a position on the grid rather than imagining that one particular living cell travels.

When it applies

The demonstration uses a finite grid. Edge treatment matters, and changing to another birth/survival rule creates a different cellular automaton.
