Autoencoders and Variational Autoencoders

Why is good reconstruction not the same as good generation?

The idea

An autoencoder learns an encoder and decoder that reconstruct inputs through a bottleneck. A variational autoencoder adds a probabilistic latent model: a prior, an approximate posterior and a decoder distribution. Its objective balances reconstruction likelihood with matching the latent posterior to the prior.

This probabilistic structure explains why a VAE can generate by sampling a latent prior. Reconstructing real inputs and decoding random prior samples are different tests. The KL term is not an arbitrary decoration; it arises from the variational lower-bound construction.

Read the relationship

ELBO=E_q[log pθ(x|z)]−KL(qφ(z|x)‖p(z))

p(z) is the latent prior, qφ the approximate posterior and pθ(x|z) the decoder distribution. Maximising this bound differs from minimising any chosen pixel error.

Work through an example

  1. Encode a dataset into a two-dimensional latent space and decode each encoded point.
  2. Then draw fresh points from the specified prior and decode those too.
  3. Good reconstructions with poor prior samples show that the two tests have exposed different properties.

When it applies

Distributional assumptions and likelihood scaling matter. A low-dimensional latent picture does not establish that each axis has a unique physical meaning.

Check your understanding

Can low reconstruction error alone prove that prior sampling generates realistic data?

No. The encoded data may occupy regions not representative of prior samples.
