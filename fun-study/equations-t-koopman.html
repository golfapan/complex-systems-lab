Koopman Operators, Observables and DMD

How can a nonlinear system have a linear description?

The idea

The Koopman viewpoint advances measurement functions rather than directly making the state equation linear. Given a rule g for measuring a state, compose it with the flow: measure after evolving. This operation is linear in the measurement function even when the state dynamics are nonlinear.

The trade-off is that the space of observables is generally infinite-dimensional. Dynamic mode decomposition, or DMD, fits a finite matrix to paired snapshots. Its modes can approximate useful timescales, but a finite fit is not automatically an exact, global Koopman representation.

Read the relationship

(Kᵗg)(x)=g(Φᵗ(x)); Kᵗ(ag+bh)=aKᵗg+bKᵗh; DMD: X′≈AX

g and h are observable functions, a and b scalars, and Φᵗ the state flow. Linearity refers to the function space.

Work through an example

  1. For the map x⁺=x², choose g₁(x)=x and obtain Kg₁=x².
  2. Adding g₂=x² is not enough: Kg₂=x⁴, which introduces another observable.
  3. Repeating creates x⁸ and further powers, illustrating why a one-state nonlinear map can require an infinite linear observable description.

When it applies

Observable choice, sampling, noise and finite data affect spectral estimates. Reconstruction accuracy does not establish global closure, reliable extrapolation or control guarantees.

Check your understanding

Does Koopman linearity let you add any two original state trajectories?

No. It is linearity in observables, not the original state's superposition principle.
