Training as a Dynamical System

What evolves when a neural network learns?

The idea

During training, the changing state includes model parameters and any optimiser memory. Full-batch gradient descent is a discrete map. Small-step continuous approximations can reveal timescales and damping, but are not exact descriptions of every training algorithm.

Momentum adds memory, SGD adds sampling randomness, and schedules alter the update over time. Distinguish the parameter trajectory from a model's forward hidden-state trajectory. Also distinguish stable optimisation, low training loss and successful generalisation: improving one does not establish the others.

Read the relationship

θₙ₊₁=θₙ−η∇L(θₙ); vₙ₊₁=βvₙ−η∇L(θₙ); θₙ₊₁=θₙ+vₙ₊₁

θ contains weights, v optimiser memory, η the step size and β a momentum coefficient. Their interpretation is mathematical, not necessarily a physical velocity.

Work through an example

  1. For L(θ)=5θ²/2, ordinary gradient descent multiplies θ by 1−5η.
  2. At η=0.1 the factor is 0.5; at η=0.3 it is −0.5. Both converge, but one oscillates.
  3. At η=0.5 the factor is −1.5, so training diverges on the same simple loss.

When it applies

Specific Nesterov continuous limits depend on algorithmic scaling. Adam and stochastic training do not inherit every conclusion of a fixed smooth gradient flow.

Check your understanding

May a decreasing continuous-limit loss justify any practical learning rate?

No. The omitted finite-step effects can determine whether the actual updates remain stable.
