Neural ODEs and Continuous Depth

What if a network predicts a rate of change rather than the next state?

The idea

A Neural ODE parameterises a hidden-state derivative with a neural network. An ODE solver integrates that rate to the desired time, then a decoder uses the resulting state. The learned object is a vector field, not simply a table of final outputs.

Residual updates resemble Euler steps, providing a helpful bridge. Continuous models additionally specify intermediate times and solver error control. The number of network evaluations depends on the solver and dynamics, so a standard residual network is not automatically an exact continuous flow.

Read the relationship

ż=fθ(z,t); z(T)=ODESolve(fθ,z₀,0,T); Euler: zₙ₊₁=zₙ+hfθ(zₙ,tₙ)

z is the hidden state, θ the vector-field parameters and T physical time or continuous depth. θ is normally fixed during one forward solve.

Work through an example

  1. Use fθ(z)=θz with z₀=1 and target z(1)=2.
  2. An exact continuous solve requires θ=ln2.
  3. One Euler step with h=1 instead requires θ=1. Solver approximation can therefore change the fitted parameter.

When it applies

Hidden coordinates need not have physical meaning. Basic initial-value models do not automatically incorporate later observations; stiffness, topology and gradient accuracy also require attention.

Check your understanding

If fθ is zero everywhere, is the final state zero?

No. A zero derivative leaves the initial state unchanged: z(T)=z₀.
