Physics-Informed Neural Networks

What if a network represents a solution and is penalised for breaking its equation?

The idea

A PINN usually maps coordinates to a candidate solution uθ. Automatic differentiation computes its derivatives, which are substituted into the differential equation to form a residual. Training combines that residual with data and initial or boundary conditions.

The learned object is a solution function, unlike a Neural ODE's learned rate of change. Soft constraints require balancing: a small residual on sampled points does not mean the full physical problem is solved everywhere. Scales, sampling, optimisation and independent testing determine what the result supports.

Read the relationship

Rθ=∂ₜuθ−κ∂ₓₓuθ; L=Ldata+λr mean|Rθ|²+λb Lboundary+λi Linitial

uθ is the candidate field. Coordinate derivatives form the PDE residual; parameter gradients update the network during training.

Work through an example

  1. For zero-boundary heat flow from sin x with κ=1, try u=e⁻ᵗsin x.
  2. Its time derivative and second spatial derivative are both −e⁻ᵗsin x, giving zero residual.
  3. The zero function also has zero PDE and boundary residual, but fails the initial condition. Residual alone cannot select the required solution.

When it applies

Small training residual is not a general error guarantee. Stiff, oscillatory or transport-dominated problems can be hard to optimise, and inverse parameters still need identifiability.

Check your understanding

Can weakly weighted initial conditions lead to the wrong physical solution?

Yes. A convenient low-residual solution can be favoured even though it fails the specified initial data.
