Preferences, RLHF, DPO and Verifiable Rewards

What kind of supervision does a preference provide?

The idea

A preference compares candidate responses rather than supplying one exact target. RLHF often learns a reward model and then optimises a policy against it. Direct Preference Optimisation uses a preference-based objective to adjust a policy more directly under its modelling assumptions.

Verifiable rewards use externally checkable outcomes, such as whether a program passes tests. Preference, reward and truth are different signals. A policy can exploit incomplete tests or a flawed reward model, so independent task success and side effects must remain visible.

Read the relationship

Preference record=(prompt, preferred response, less-preferred response)

Many methods also compare or constrain the policy relative to a reference model. The precise objective determines what the preference data imply.

Work through an example

  1. Suppose a coding answer passes every provided unit test.
  2. It may still fail unseen cases if the tests cover only easy inputs.
  3. Training only to maximise that test reward can encourage exploiting omissions instead of general correctness.

When it applies

Human preference is not a proof of factual accuracy. RLHF, DPO and outcome rewards have different assumptions and failure modes and should not be treated as one algorithm.

Check your understanding

Why evaluate reward and independent task success separately?

A proxy score can improve while the intended behaviour fails or develops unwanted side effects.
