Observations, Inference and Uncertainty

How should noisy measurements change what you believe about a model?

The idea

Inference connects unknown quantities to measured data through an observation model. Uncertain initial conditions, sensor noise, unknown parameters and model discrepancy are separate sources of uncertainty. Keeping them explicit makes the meaning of an estimate easier to assess.

Bayes' rule combines a likelihood with a prior to obtain a posterior. The answer remains conditional on those modelling assumptions. Narrow uncertainty does not rescue a wrong observation model, and data that cannot distinguish parameters cannot suddenly identify them because an optimiser runs longer.

Read the relationship

p(θ|y)∝p(y|θ)p(θ); posterior variance=(1/τ²+1/σ²)⁻¹

For the scalar example, θ has Gaussian prior variance τ² and y=θ+ε with independent Gaussian noise variance σ².

Work through an example

  1. Use prior θ~N(0,4), observe y=1 and set noise variance to one.
  2. Posterior precision is 1/4+1=1.25, so posterior variance is 0.8.
  3. The posterior mean is 0.8×(0/4+1)=0.8: a compromise weighted by uncertainty.

When it applies

The closed-form calculation relies on linear Gaussian assumptions. Correlated errors, multimodality and structural model error require a more suitable analysis.

Check your understanding

May the same reading be counted twice as independent evidence?

No. That would artificially increase precision; the dependence must be represented.
