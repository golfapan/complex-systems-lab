Bayesian estimation and posterior prediction

How can a model keep uncertainty about a parameter while still predicting?

The idea

Bayesian inference combines a prior distribution with the likelihood to obtain a posterior distribution. Instead of selecting only one parameter, posterior prediction averages the predictions associated with different parameter values, weighted by their posterior plausibility. Parameter uncertainty therefore contributes to uncertainty about a future observation.

A conjugate prior makes this update especially transparent. A Beta prior for a Bernoulli probability remains Beta after observing successes and failures. Its two shape parameters act as algebraic contributions to the update, but their interpretation must follow the chosen prior; they are not necessarily literal earlier observations.

Read the relationship

p|D~Beta(α+k,β+n−k); P(Xnew=1|D)=(α+k)/(α+β+n)

α and β are positive prior shape parameters; k successes and n−k failures come from conditionally independent trials.

Work through an example

  1. Start with the uniform Beta(1,1) prior and observe four heads in four tosses.
  2. The posterior is Beta(5,1), rather than a point mass at one.
  3. The next-head probability is the posterior mean 5/6≈0.8333, while the MLE is one.

When it applies

Posterior conclusions depend on the likelihood and prior. A credible interval has a different interpretation from a repeated-sampling confidence interval.

Check your understanding

Would collecting more simulated posterior draws make the actual parameter uncertainty disappear?

No. More draws can reduce numerical error in computing the posterior, but do not supply additional observations about the coin.
