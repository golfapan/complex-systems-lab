Complex Systems Lab / 03

A Lenia-type living field

What changes if a cell can be partly active rather than simply alive or dead?

Run this experiment →

The idea

A continuous activity field senses its neighbourhood through a ring-shaped averaging kernel. A nonlinear growth function turns that sensed value into an increase or decrease of activity. The combination can support moving or changing structures, even though every location follows the same rule.

The rule depends on a whole neighbourhood, not only on the nearest cell. A kernel's range and the growth curve play different roles.

Read the relationship

U=K*A A⁺=clip(A+Δt G(U),0,1)

A is activity, K is a normalized neighbourhood kernel, and G is a growth response. Convolution performs the sensing step; it is not itself growth.

Try this

  1. Change the preferred neighbourhood activity and restart from the same seed. Watch whether a compact structure spreads, collapses or develops new boundaries.

When it applies

This is a single-channel Lenia-type model. Clipping is an explicit projection, so continuous-valued activity does not make every update smooth.
