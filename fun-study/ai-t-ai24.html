Diffusion, Scores and Flow Matching

Which part is learned, and which part generates a sample?

The idea

Diffusion models learn denoising or score information at multiple noise levels. Sampling then follows a corresponding reverse process. Flow Matching instead learns velocities for a designed path between distributions, generating by integrating the learned field.

The training target and sampling solver are two layers of design. A denoising objective is not itself an ODE update, and replacing a solver can change cost and quality without retraining the network. Keep the prediction object, time convention and numerical approximation explicit.

Read the relationship

Denoising example: L=E‖ε−εθ(xₜ,t)‖²; flow sampling: dx/dt=vθ(x,t)

ε is sampled perturbation noise, εθ its prediction, and vθ a learned transport velocity. Their relation depends on the chosen process and parameterisation.

Work through an example

  1. Hold one trained velocity field fixed.
  2. Generate using a coarse solver, then a finer one with the same starting sample.
  3. Different outputs can reflect integration error rather than a change in learned weights; compare quality against computation.

When it applies

Similar-looking generation paths need not have identical stochastic laws. A score, clean-sample prediction and velocity are not interchangeable without the appropriate formulas.

Check your understanding

Does changing sampling temperature or solver step count usually retrain the model?

No. These normally change inference; their effects should be evaluated separately from learning.
