Self-Supervision and Representation Learning

Where do labels come from when nobody labels the examples?

The idea

Self-supervised learning constructs a prediction task from the data itself: hide content, predict a later item, or relate two views. It still requires a designed objective. The training signal has moved from manual labels into how the problem is constructed.

Data augmentation defines which changes a representation should tolerate. A transformation useful for one task can erase critical information for another. Judge representations through independent downstream tasks and controlled alternatives, not only through the pretraining loss.

Read the relationship

z=fθ(augment(x)); train with a predictive or contrastive objective

x is an example, augment a chosen transformation and z a representation. The objective determines which relationships between examples matter.

Work through an example

  1. Two crops of a photograph can preserve the identity of the same object.
  2. Treating them as related views encourages features robust to the crop.
  3. In a medical task, a crop might remove the lesion, so imposing the same invariance could be harmful.

When it applies

Different views of one underlying object can leak across train and test splits. Low contrastive loss does not by itself establish causal or universally useful features.

Check your understanding

Does self-supervised mean that no human modelling choices are involved?

No. Objectives, augmentations, datasets and evaluation all involve choices.
