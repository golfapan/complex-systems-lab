Hamiltonian Dynamics and Symplectic Structure

How can an energy-like function generate a direction of motion?

The idea

Hamiltonian mechanics works with positions q and their canonical momenta p. Differentiating a scalar H in paired directions produces the velocity field. For a time-independent H, the two contributions to its change cancel, so the continuous evolution preserves H.

The symplectic structure concerns the geometry of these paired coordinates, not just one energy number. Energy preservation, reversibility, volume preservation and symplecticity are related properties but are not equivalent. A method can perform well on one and poorly on another.

Read the relationship

q̇=∂H/∂p; ṗ=−∂H/∂q; H=p²/(2m)+kq²/2

p=mq̇ for this simple mechanical model. More generally momentum is defined from the Lagrangian, so replacing it with velocity requires justification.

Work through an example

  1. With m=k=1, the equations are q̇=p and ṗ=−q.
  2. At (q,p)=(1,0), the direction is (0,−1), tangent to the circle H=1/2.
  3. Compute Ḣ=q p+p(−q)=0. Adding damping −cp changes this to Ḣ=−cp².

When it applies

An explicitly time-dependent H need not be conserved. Noncanonical coordinates require the appropriate geometric formulation, and numerical integration may introduce energy error.

Check your understanding

If a numerical method has small energy error, must it be symplectic?

No. Symplecticity constrains the full update map, not merely one scalar diagnostic.
