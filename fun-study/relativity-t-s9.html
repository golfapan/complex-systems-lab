General relativity: the geometry becomes unknown

What changes when spacetime itself must be solved for?

The idea

In special relativity, the background Minkowski geometry is fixed. General relativity makes the metric a field whose curvature is related to matter and energy. The gravitational field equations are therefore not merely another force term added to a particle trajectory; they determine part of the stage on which motion takes place.

There are two linked problems. Given a suitable metric, geodesic equations predict test-body motion. For a self-consistent gravitating system, matter equations and gravitational field equations must be solved together, with constraints and boundary or initial data. This distinction separates movement in a given field from determining the field itself.

Read the relationship

Gμν+Λgμν=(8πG/c⁴)Tμν

Gμν is the Einstein curvature tensor, gμν the metric, Λ the cosmological constant, and Tμν the stress-energy tensor; G without indices is Newton's constant.

Work through an example

  1. First consider a specified exterior spacetime around a spherical gravitating body.
  2. A sufficiently small test object follows a geodesic determined by that metric, without changing it appreciably.
  3. To model the gravitating body's evolution or comparable interacting masses, the geometry can no longer simply be held fixed.

When it applies

A weak-field Newtonian approximation has a domain of validity. Full solutions require coordinate choices, constraints, and appropriate treatment of matter and singularities.

Check your understanding

Is solving a particle's geodesic in a given metric the same as solving Einstein's field equations?

No. The former advances a path in known geometry; the latter determines geometry from the complete gravitational problem.
