Designing a small investigation

How do you turn a broad question about randomness into a testable project?

The idea

Start by naming a model and a prediction object. A trajectory, a histogram, a mean, and a correlation function require different comparisons. Then specify the initial preparation, boundaries, parameters, observation time, and quantities to measure. That turns a broad discussion into a claim another person can check.

Keep numerical error, sampling error, and model mismatch separate. More simulated repetitions reduce sampling error; smaller time steps may reduce discretization error; neither automatically repairs a wrong physical model. A good report compares an independent analytical prediction where available, records uncertainties, and explains what remains outside its scope.

Read the relationship

Model → preparation → prediction object → observable → error checks

For a symmetric n-step walk, E[X]=0 and E[X²]=n a²; the finite-step endpoint law is binomial on the walk's lattice.

Work through an example

  1. Fix n=100 and step length a=1, then simulate many independent walkers.
  2. Compare their endpoint histogram with exact binomial probabilities and their mean squared displacement with 100.
  3. Increase walker count while keeping n fixed to test sampling precision; change n separately to test time scaling.

When it applies

A matching histogram is evidence for the tested observable and parameter range, not proof that the model captures every mechanism or time correlation.

Check your understanding

What does increasing the number of walkers change, compared with increasing the number of steps?

The first improves estimation at a chosen time; the second changes the time horizon and the target distribution.
