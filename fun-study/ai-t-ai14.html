ResNets and Transfer Learning

When should a learned visual representation be reused?

The idea

A residual network preserves an identity or projection route alongside a learned transformation, making deep representation changes easier to manage. Transfer learning reuses representations already fitted to another dataset, rather than assuming every task needs training from scratch.

Freezing a backbone, fitting a new head and fine-tuning the whole model are different experiments. Their usefulness depends on data size, domain similarity and evaluation. More trainable weights can improve adaptation while also increasing overfitting and resource requirements.

Read the relationship

y=x+F(x), or y=P(x)+F(x) when shapes change

F is the learned residual branch and P a projection that makes dimensions compatible. A backbone produces features; a head converts them to task outputs.

Work through an example

  1. Freeze a pretrained image backbone and train a small classifier head as a baseline.
  2. Check validation performance, then unfreeze selected layers with controlled settings.
  3. Keep the adaptation only if held-out performance improves; lower training loss alone is not enough.

When it applies

Pretraining can introduce overlap with evaluation data. Input normalisation, label mapping and domain differences must be checked before interpreting gains.

Check your understanding

Is a frozen backbone identical to training a new network from random initialisation?

No. It brings fixed learned features and their inherited strengths and biases into the new task.
