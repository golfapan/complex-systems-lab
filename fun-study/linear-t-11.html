Symmetric Matrices, Positive Definiteness and Quadratic Forms

How can a matrix tell us whether every direction curves uphill?

The idea

A quadratic form xᵀHx measures a direction-dependent energy or curvature. For a real symmetric matrix, an orthonormal eigenbasis separates that form into weighted squares. Positive eigenvalues make every nonzero direction contribute positively; mixed signs reveal both uphill and downhill directions.

This structure connects optimisation, covariance and mechanical energy. Positive definite means positivity of the quadratic form, not positivity of every matrix entry. Positive semidefinite allows flat directions, which can represent uncertainty restricted to a subspace or directions with no second-order curvature.

Read the relationship

H=Hᵀ, H positive definite ⇔ xᵀHx>0 for x≠0 H=QΛQᵀ; positive definite H admits H=LLᵀ

Q is orthogonal and Λ lists real eigenvalues. L in the Cholesky factorisation is lower triangular under the usual convention.

Work through an example

  1. Let H=[[2,1],[1,2]].
  2. The direction (1,1) has eigenvalue 3 and (1,−1) has eigenvalue 1.
  3. Both are positive, so f(x)=xᵀHx/2 has a unique global minimum at zero and elliptical level sets.

When it applies

For a general nonlinear function, a positive-definite Hessian at a stationary point establishes a local minimum. Semidefiniteness alone may leave the classification undecided.

Check your understanding

Can a positive-definite matrix contain negative entries?

Yes. For example [[2,−1],[−1,2]] has positive eigenvalues 1 and 3. Definiteness concerns all directional quadratic values.
