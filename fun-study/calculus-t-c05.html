Integration Methods and Infinite Accumulation

How do we choose an integration method—and decide whether an infinite integral exists?

The idea

Substitution reverses the chain rule; integration by parts reverses the product rule. The aim is to reveal structure and produce an easier integral, not merely to change notation. For definite integrals, a substitution must change the limits as well as the expression and differential.

An improper integral adds another layer: first integrate over a finite, nonsingular region, then take a limit. Infinite length does not force infinite accumulation. A rapidly shrinking tail can contribute a finite amount, while a finite interval can still have divergent accumulation near a strong singularity.

Read the relationship

∫u dv=uv−∫v du ∫₁∞x^(−p)dx converges exactly when p>1 Arc length: s=∫√(x′(t)²+y′(t)²)dt

The arc-length integrand is speed, not position. For an improper integral, infinity represents a limiting process rather than an ordinary endpoint.

Work through an example

  1. Compute ∫₁ᴮx⁻²dx=1−1/B.
  2. Letting B tend to infinity gives the finite value 1.
  3. By contrast, ∫₁ᴮx⁻¹dx=ln B grows without bound, so that improper integral diverges.

When it applies

At an internal singularity, the two sides must converge separately. A symmetric principal value is a different concept; cancellation of divergent pieces does not establish an ordinary integral.

Check your understanding

When does ∫₀¹x^(−p)dx converge?

Exactly when p<1. The condition near zero is the opposite of the tail condition at infinity.
