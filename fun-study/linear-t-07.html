Projection and Least Squares

If exact agreement is impossible, what is the closest achievable prediction?

The idea

When b lies outside A's column space, no x makes Ax=b. Least squares selects the reachable output closest to b in Euclidean distance. The best residual is perpendicular to every reachable direction: moving the prediction a little within the column space cannot improve the fit to first order.

That geometric condition yields the normal equations, connecting projection to calculus-based optimisation. Full column rank makes the coefficient solution unique. With redundant columns, several coefficient vectors can make the same best prediction, so additional criteria such as minimum norm or regularisation may be needed.

Read the relationship

minx ‖Ax−b‖₂² Aᵀ(Ax̂−b)=0 For full column rank: P=A(AᵀA)⁻¹Aᵀ

p=Ax̂ is the projected output and r=b−p the residual. P is symmetric and idempotent: Pᵀ=P and P²=P.

Work through an example

  1. Fit a single constant c to observations 1, 2 and 3.
  2. With A=(1,1,1)ᵀ, the normal equation becomes 3c=6, so c=2.
  3. The residual (−1,0,1) is perpendicular to A, and its squared length is 2.

When it applies

The normal equations are valuable for understanding, but forming AᵀA can square the condition number. QR or SVD is often a better computational route.

Check your understanding

Is the best prediction necessarily the same thing as a unique coefficient vector?

No. Rank-deficient A can produce one closest output from many different coefficient vectors differing by nullspace components.
