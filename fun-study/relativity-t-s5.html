Relativity / 05

Light cones, causality, and the twins

Which events can influence one another, and which paths accumulate different times?

The idea

The interval divides event separations into timelike, lightlike, and spacelike classes. Timelike or lightlike connections lie within the causal cone of an event; spacelike-separated events cannot be joined by a signal traveling at or below c in special relativity. Different frames may reverse the time order of spacelike events without reversing a possible causal influence.

The twin example compares complete worldlines, not two perfectly symmetric inertial situations. A traveling clock must change its path to return. Its accumulated proper time differs from a clock that remains on the inertial path between the reunion events. The result can be calculated directly without treating acceleration as a mysterious clock-damaging effect.

Read the relationship

s²>0: timelike; s²=0: lightlike; s²<0: spacelike; τ=∫√(1−u(t)²/c²)dt

The integral is evaluated along the clock's worldline using one consistent inertial-frame coordinate time.

Work through an example

  1. Idealize an outward journey of five lab years and a return of five lab years, each at speed 0.6c.
  2. Each travel segment accumulates five times √(1−0.36)=four years.
  3. The stay-at-home clock reads ten years and the traveling clock eight, neglecting a brief turnaround duration.

When it applies

A realistic finite acceleration segment contributes its own proper-time integral. Entanglement correlations do not supply a controllable exception to relativistic signaling limits.

Check your understanding

Does the traveler simply use one inertial frame for the entire out-and-back trip?

No. Reversing direction changes the traveler's inertial description; the complete paths are not symmetric.
