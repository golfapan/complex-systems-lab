Randomness and Regularity / 08

Different state spaces preserve different structures

Why are probability vectors, quantum amplitudes, and spacetime coordinates not interchangeable?

The idea

All three descriptions use vectors and matrices, but their entries mean different things. A probability vector contains nonnegative weights summing to one. A quantum state contains complex amplitudes whose squared norm is one. A spacetime vector records event coordinates with a metric that has different time and space signs.

Their transformations preserve different structures. Markov evolution preserves probability normalization, unitary evolution preserves a complex inner product, and Lorentz transformations preserve spacetime intervals. A shared matrix notation is useful for comparison, but it cannot erase these differences in meaning or turn all three operations into ordinary Euclidean rotation.

Read the relationship

Markov: pnext=Pp; quantum: ψt=Uψ₀ with U†U=I; relativity: ΛᵀηΛ=η

The displayed Markov convention uses column probability vectors and a nonnegative matrix whose columns sum to one.

Work through an example

  1. Compare a 50/50 classical mixture of |0⟩ and |1⟩ with |+⟩=(|0⟩+|1⟩)/√2.
  2. Both give equal zero/one probabilities in that measurement basis.
  3. In the plus/minus basis, the mixture remains 50/50, but |+⟩ gives plus with certainty; phase coherence contains additional information.

When it applies

A list of probabilities for one measurement does not completely specify a quantum state. Spacetime coordinates are neither probabilities nor amplitudes.

Check your understanding

Can identical probabilities in one basis prove two quantum preparations are identical?

No. Measurements in another basis can distinguish preparations that differ in coherence.
