Posterior computation, Monte Carlo, and MCMC

How can you calculate with a posterior whose normalizing constant is unknown?

The idea

Monte Carlo approximates expectations with averages of draws. Markov chain Monte Carlo constructs a chain whose stationary distribution is the desired posterior. The next state depends on the current state through a transition rule; carefully designed transitions can use ratios in which an unknown normalizing constant cancels.

A valid stationary distribution is only part of a practical sampling method. The chain must explore it, including separated modes and tails. Correlated draws carry less information than equally many independent draws. More computation can reduce Monte Carlo error in a posterior summary without reducing the posterior uncertainty caused by limited data.

Read the relationship

E[g(θ)|D]≈Σm g(θ(m))/M; symmetric-proposal acceptance=min(1,p̃(θ′)/p̃(θ))

p̃ is an unnormalized target density. Nonsymmetric proposals require the additional reverse/forward proposal-density ratio.

Work through an example

  1. The current state's unnormalized density is two, and a proposed state's is one.
  2. With a symmetric proposal, accept the move with probability one half.
  3. If the proposed state's unnormalized target density is six instead, acceptance is one; the shared unknown normalization has canceled in both ratios.

When it applies

Acceptance rate alone does not establish convergence. Inspect multiple chains, mixing, effective sample size, Monte Carlo error, and sampler-specific diagnostics.

Check your understanding

Current density is four, proposed density one, and a uniform draw is 0.3. Accept?

No. The acceptance probability is 0.25, and 0.3 exceeds it, so retain the current state.
