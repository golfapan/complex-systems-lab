Optimal Control, Value Functions and HJB

How do you choose an action for its future consequences?

The idea

Optimal control combines dynamics, an objective and constraints. An input that immediately moves towards the target may still waste energy or violate limits. The objective often accumulates running cost and a terminal cost, so actions must be judged over a horizon.

A value function records the best future cost from each state. Dynamic programming converts consistency of optimal future decisions into a Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation. Trajectory optimisation usually solves a different output problem: one suitable trajectory from a specified initial condition, not an optimal feedback law everywhere.

Read the relationship

J=∫ₜᵀℓ(x,u)ds+φ(x(T)); −Vₜ=minᵤ[ℓ+∇V·f]

V is optimal cost-to-go, ℓ running cost and φ terminal cost. The minimisation must respect the admissible inputs.

Work through an example

  1. Consider ẋ=u with infinite-horizon cost ∫₀∞(x²+u²)dt; try V=x².
  2. The HJB expression becomes minᵤ[x²+u²+2xu]=minᵤ(u+x)², so u*=−x.
  3. Then x=e⁻ᵗx₀. For x₀=1 the cost is ∫₀∞2e⁻²ᵗdt=1, matching V(1).

When it applies

The example has no saturation, delay or noise. General HJB problems can be nonsmooth and high-dimensional; local or nominal solutions need not provide global guarantees.

Check your understanding

Is an optimal open-loop input curve also the best response to every disturbance?

No. Feedback must adapt to the realised state rather than merely repeat a nominal plan.
