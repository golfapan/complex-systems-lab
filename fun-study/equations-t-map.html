Discrete Maps and Recurrences

How do you describe a system that changes one step at a time?

The idea

A map sends the current state to the next state. A step might mean one generation, one day, one algorithm update or one sample of a continuous process. Decide what a step represents before searching for a differential equation underneath it.

A fixed point is a state unchanged by the map. Nearby behaviour is controlled, to first order, by the derivative in one dimension or the Jacobian in several dimensions. A negative multiplier makes perturbations switch sides; its magnitude determines whether they shrink or grow. Alternation alone does not establish chaos.

Read the relationship

xₙ₊₁=F(xₙ); δxₙ₊₁≈F′(x*)δxₙ; F(x)=rx(1−x)

n counts updates, r is the map parameter, and x* satisfies F(x*)=x*. The prime differentiates with respect to state.

Work through an example

  1. Set r=2.5. The nonzero fixed point is x*=1−1/r=0.6.
  2. Its multiplier is 2.5(1−2×0.6)=−0.5.
  3. Starting at 0.61 gives 0.59475, then approximately 0.60256: the error changes sign and shrinks.

When it applies

Derivative tests are local. At multiplier magnitude one, higher-order analysis is needed. A discrete Logistic map is not the exact sampling of the continuous Logistic equation.

Check your understanding

What does a multiplier of −1.2 tell you?

Nearby errors alternate and grow while linearisation remains useful. The fixed point is unstable; this fact alone does not prove chaos.
