From probability density to generative models

What does it mean for a model to generate a new possible observation?

The idea

A generative model describes how data are distributed, making it possible to sample new outcomes. A latent-variable model introduces unobserved variables that help explain observed variation. To obtain an observation's probability, it integrates over possible latent values rather than choosing just one hidden explanation.

A variational autoencoder uses an encoder distribution to approximate the latent posterior and a decoder distribution to generate observations. The evidence lower bound makes likelihood learning tractable through an optimization objective. Autoregressive models, flows, and diffusion models use other constructions; sharing probability foundations does not make their training objectives identical.

Read the relationship

pθ(x)=∫pθ(x,z)dz; log pθ(x)=ELBO+DKL(q(z|x)‖pθ(z|x))

z is latent, q is an approximate posterior, and the nonnegative KL gap makes ELBO a lower bound when the expressions are well defined.

Work through an example

  1. Suppose log pθ(x)=−2 and the approximate-to-true posterior KL is 0.3.
  2. The lower bound is ELBO=−2−0.3=−2.3.
  3. If q matches the true posterior, the gap vanishes and the bound equals −2.

When it applies

An expressive network does not remove optimization and generalization errors. Better likelihood does not guarantee factual correctness of generated content.

Check your understanding

Does a VAE encoder merely output one deterministic compressed vector?

Generally it outputs parameters of a latent distribution; sampling and posterior uncertainty are part of the model.
