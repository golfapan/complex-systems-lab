Calculus of Variations: Optimising a Function

What changes when the unknown is a whole path rather than a number?

The idea

A functional assigns a number to an entire function. To test a candidate path, perturb it by another admissible function and examine the first-order change. Requiring this variation to vanish for every permitted perturbation leads to an equation for the path itself.

Integration by parts moves derivatives off the perturbation, producing both an interior Euler–Lagrange equation and boundary terms. Fixed endpoint values can remove those terms; free values produce further conditions. A stationary path need not minimise the functional, just as a zero gradient need not identify a minimum in ordinary optimisation.

Read the relationship

J[y]=∫ₐᵇL(x,y,y′)dx yε=y+εη ∂L/∂y−d/dx(∂L/∂y′)=0

η is an allowed perturbation function. Fixed endpoint values require η(a)=η(b)=0. The functional's input is the entire y, not just one value y(x).

Work through an example

  1. Minimise J=∫₀¹(y′)²dx with y(0)=0 and y(1)=1.
  2. Euler–Lagrange gives y″=0, selecting the candidate y=x.
  3. Cauchy–Schwarz also gives J≥(∫₀¹y′dx)²=1, achieved by y=x; this confirms a global minimum.

When it applies

The displayed derivation needs appropriate smoothness and admissible variations. Free or moving endpoints change boundary conditions; stationarity alone does not prove minimality.

Check your understanding

Why cannot the boundary term simply be deleted for every problem?

It disappears only when the permitted variations or other boundary conditions make it vanish. Otherwise it supplies necessary endpoint conditions.
