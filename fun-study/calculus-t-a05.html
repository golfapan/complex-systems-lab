Differential Geometry: Measuring Shape Without Confusing Coordinates

What is the difference between looking curved in space and having curved internal geometry?

The idea

Curvature measures how a unit tangent changes per unit arc length, separating a curve's shape from how quickly it is traversed. A surface metric similarly converts changes in parameters into actual lengths and angles. A poor coordinate chart can fail even when the underlying curve or surface remains perfectly regular.

Intrinsic geometry concerns measurements made within a surface. A sheet rolled into a cylinder bends in surrounding space but can preserve local lengths and angles; a sphere cannot be flattened this way without distortion. Differential forms provide a further language for oriented integration, connecting local differentiation with boundary integrals through generalised Stokes.

Read the relationship

Curve curvature: κ=|r′×r″|/|r′|³ Surface metric: ds²=E du²+2F du dv+G dv² Generalised Stokes: ∫M dω=∫∂M ω

E=ru·ru, F=ru·rv and G=rv·rv. A regular surface chart requires independent tangent vectors ru and rv.

Work through an example

  1. Parametrise a cylinder by r(u,v)=(Rcosu,Rsinu,v).
  2. Dot products give E=R², F=0 and G=1.
  3. With ξ=Ru, the metric becomes ds²=dξ²+dv², locally the plane's metric despite the cylinder's curved appearance.

When it applies

Curve curvature and Gaussian surface curvature are different quantities: a radius-R circle has curvature 1/R, while a radius-R sphere has Gaussian curvature 1/R². Geodesics need not remain globally shortest.

Check your understanding

Does a coordinate singularity prove the surface itself is singular?

No. Another regular chart may describe the same smooth region; distinguish the object from its chosen coordinates.
