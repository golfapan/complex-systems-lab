Potential wells and discrete energy levels

How do boundary conditions turn a differential equation into allowed energies?

The idea

A stationary-state calculation asks which wavefunctions reproduce themselves under the Hamiltonian up to an energy multiplier. This is an eigenvalue problem involving both a differential expression and boundary conditions. In a confined region, those conditions can select a discrete set of allowed energies rather than every numerical value.

A stationary energy state has a time-dependent overall phase but a time-independent position density. A superposition of different energy states generally has changing relative phases and an oscillating density. Linear algebra's eigenvector decomposition therefore connects directly to time-dependent quantum predictions.

Read the relationship

−(ħ²/2m)φn″+Vφn=Enφn; box: φn=√(2/L)sin(nπx/L), En=n²π²ħ²/(2mL²)

The box occupies 0<x<L with ideal infinite walls; n is a positive integer. L and the boundary conditions are part of the problem.

Work through an example

  1. For the ideal box, E₂=4E₁, so the gap is 3E₁.
  2. Prepare a superposition of n=1 and n=2 rather than a single energy eigenstate.
  3. Its interference term varies with the energy-difference frequency (E₂−E₁)/ħ=3E₁/ħ.

When it applies

Different potentials and boundary conditions produce different spectra. An infinite-wall box is a model, not a universal description of every confined particle.

Check your understanding

Does an energy eigenstate's global phase changing in time make its position density oscillate?

No. A common phase cancels in |ψ|²; relative phases between different components create interference changes.
