Probability and Statistics / 04

Discrete random variables and familiar distributions

How do you choose a distribution for counts or waiting trials?

The idea

A random variable turns an outcome into a number. A Bernoulli variable records one success or failure; a binomial counts successes in a fixed number of independent trials with the same success probability. A geometric variable counts trials until the first success. The definition of what is being counted determines the formula.

A Poisson model describes counts in a window under a particular event-process assumption. Its mean and variance are both λ. That convenient property is also a check: a count dataset with much larger variation may need a different model. Distribution names summarize assumptions rather than replace them.

Read the relationship

Binomial: P(X=k)=C(n,k)pᵏ(1−p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ; Poisson: P(X=k)=e⁻λ λᵏ/k!

n is the trial count, p is a trial's success probability, and λ is the expected count in a specified window.

Work through an example

  1. Five independent components each fail with probability 0.1. We want exactly two failures.
  2. There are C(5,2)=10 ways to choose their positions; each pattern has probability 0.1²×0.9³.
  3. The total probability is 10×0.01×0.729=0.0729, or 7.29%.

When it applies

Changing probabilities across trials or dependence between components invalidates the simple binomial calculation.

Check your understanding

What is the mean number of failures among these five components?

It is np=0.5. An expectation need not itself be a possible integer outcome.
