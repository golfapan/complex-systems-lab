Probability and Statistics / 05

Continuous distributions, density, and transformations

Why can a density exceed one without breaking probability rules?

The idea

For a continuous random variable, probability belongs to intervals and other regions, not to individual points. A density is probability per unit of the variable. Its height can exceed one provided the total area is one. The cumulative distribution function records the area accumulated up to a chosen value.

Changing variables changes how probability is packed into space. Stretching an interval reduces its density; compressing it increases density. The derivative of the inverse transformation supplies that scale correction. For nonmonotone transformations, several original points may contribute to the same transformed value.

Read the relationship

P(a≤X≤b)=∫ₐᵇfₓ(x)dx; fᵧ(y)=fₓ(g⁻¹(y))|d g⁻¹(y)/dy|

The transformation formula shown assumes a differentiable, one-to-one transformation on the relevant support.

Work through an example

  1. Let X be uniform on [0,2] seconds. Its density is 1/2 per second.
  2. The interval [0.5,1.5] has length one, so its probability is 1/2.
  3. For Y=3X, the support becomes [0,6] and the density is (1/2)(1/3)=1/6, preserving total probability.

When it applies

Always specify the support and units. A normal model may be unsuitable for a variable that cannot be negative or has heavy tails.

Check your understanding

If an interval's width halves while its probability stays fixed, what happens to its average density?

The average density doubles. Density is an amount per unit length, whereas probability is dimensionless.
