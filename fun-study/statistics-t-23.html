Probability and Statistics / 23

Experiments, A/B tests, and causal questions

What changes when you actively change a treatment?

The idea

Prediction asks what outcome is likely given observed features. A causal question asks how outcomes would differ under an intervention. Potential outcomes describe the same unit under treatment and control, although normally only one can be observed. Random assignment supports comparison by breaking systematic treatment selection in the experiment.

Random sampling and random assignment solve different problems. Sampling helps connect a sample to a population; assignment helps compare treatment effects within a study. An experiment among volunteers can have good internal causal identification yet still require evidence before generalizing to other people. Attrition and interference also need attention.

Read the relationship

ATE=E[Y(1)−Y(0)]; SE(Ȳ₁−Ȳ₀)=√(s₁²/n₁+s₀²/n₀)

The standard-error formula assumes independent groups. Paired, clustered, or dependent designs need their corresponding variance analysis.

Work through an example

  1. Two randomized groups each contain 100 observations, with means twelve and ten and standard deviations five.
  2. The estimated difference is two and its standard error is √(25/100+25/100)≈0.7071.
  3. A large-sample 95% interval is about [0.614,3.386], before considering study-specific complications.

When it applies

Randomization does not automatically fix noncompliance, missing outcomes, contamination, or measurement errors. Define the intervention and target population precisely.

Check your understanding

People who voluntarily train score higher. Is the score difference necessarily the training effect?

No. Motivation, prior skill, or other factors could influence both participation and scores.
