Normalisation, Residual Paths and Regularisation

What changes when a layer can preserve its input directly?

The idea

A residual connection adds an identity route around a learned transformation. This changes how information and gradients travel through depth. Batch normalisation and layer normalisation instead rescale selected collections of activations; the axes used for their statistics matter.

Dropout, weight decay and early stopping influence training or generalisation in different ways. Do not merge them into one mechanism just because each can reduce overfitting. Some layers also behave differently during training and inference, so evaluation requires more than simply avoiding gradient calculations.

Read the relationship

hₗ₊₁=hₗ+Fₗ(hₗ); Jacobian=I+Jꜰ

I is the identity matrix and Jꜰ the Jacobian of the residual branch. The two branches must have compatible output shapes.

Work through an example

  1. If F is near zero, the block can still pass h through its identity branch.
  2. BatchNorm may use current batch statistics in training and running statistics in evaluation.
  3. LayerNorm generally computes statistics within each example's feature representation, so it does not perform the same averaging.

When it applies

Residual paths do not prevent every gradient problem. Normalisation, dropout and regularisation must be tested with the intended data, mode and architecture.

Check your understanding

Does disabling gradient recording automatically switch off dropout?

No. Evaluation mode and gradient-recording mode are separate settings.
