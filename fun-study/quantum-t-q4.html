Schrödinger evolution and probability flow

How can quantum evolution be deterministic while measurements are probabilistic?

The idea

Between specified measurement interactions, a closed system's state obeys the Schrödinger equation. A self-adjoint Hamiltonian generates unitary evolution, preserving the state's norm and hence total probability. The equation determines the evolving amplitudes, while the measurement rule translates the resulting state into outcome probabilities.

Energy differences change relative phases and can make interference vary with time. A position-density picture also has a probability current, linking quantum mechanics to continuity equations. Density alone does not determine that current, because spatial phase gradients matter. This is another reason not to discard complex amplitudes too early.

Read the relationship

iħ∂t|ψ⟩=H|ψ⟩; U(t)=exp(−iHt/ħ); j=(ħ/m)Im(ψ*∇ψ)

The ordinary exponential shown assumes time-independent H. The current formula is for a nonrelativistic particle with a real scalar potential and no vector potential.

Work through an example

  1. Take H=diag(E₀,E₁) and initial state (|0⟩+|1⟩)/√2.
  2. At time t the components acquire phases e^(−iE₀t/ħ) and e^(−iE₁t/ħ).
  3. Their relative phase changes at rate (E₁−E₀)/ħ, so a suitable interference measurement oscillates even though basis populations remain equal.

When it applies

An open system or a specified measurement interaction needs an expanded model. Unitarity does not by itself select a single measurement outcome.

Check your understanding

Can two wavefunctions have equal |ψ|² but different probability currents?

Yes. Different spatial phase gradients can produce different currents while leaving the instantaneous density unchanged.
