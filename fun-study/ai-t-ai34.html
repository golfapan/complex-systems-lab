Scaling Laws and Compute Budgets

How do you compare more parameters with more training data?

The idea

Scaling laws fit empirical relationships within specified model families, data mixtures and training regimes. They can guide resource allocation, but are not universal laws of intelligence. Model size and token count should be varied together when compute is constrained.

A fit must distinguish observations from extrapolation. Holding out an unseen scale tests whether the relationship predicts something beyond the fitted points. Training loss, validation loss, task performance and deployment value may improve differently, so choose the target before recommending a budget.

Read the relationship

Illustrative fit: L(N,D)=L∞+aN^(−α)+bD^(−β)

N is parameter count, D token count, and the coefficients and exponents are fitted under a stated experimental regime.

Work through an example

  1. Train several small models with controlled data and compute accounting.
  2. Fit a trend using only some model sizes, then predict a held-out size.
  3. Compare that prediction with the observed validation loss and report its error, rather than presenting the extrapolated curve as measured fact.

When it applies

A single large-model score cannot identify several scaling exponents. Changes in data quality, architecture or optimisation can move the regime.

Check your understanding

Does a lower validation language loss establish better performance on every downstream task?

No. The connection must be tested for the particular capabilities and costs of interest.
