Structured Dynamics: Hamiltonian and Lagrangian Networks

Can known structure restrict what a network is allowed to learn?

The idea

A Hamiltonian Neural Network learns a scalar Hθ and derives motion using Hamilton's equations, rather than independently fitting every velocity component. This embeds a conservative structure and reduces the freedom of the learned vector field.

A Lagrangian model instead learns Lθ and uses Euler–Lagrange relations. Coordinates and regularity matter: a singular velocity Hessian cannot simply be inverted to obtain acceleration. A useful inductive bias still needs data to identify the correct dynamics, and arbitrary visual features are not automatically canonical coordinates.

Read the relationship

q̇=∂pHθ; ṗ=−∂qHθ; example Hθ=(a q²+b p²)/2

q and p are canonical variables. Learning a conserved scalar does not by itself establish that it equals the true physical energy.

Work through an example

  1. The example produces q̇=bp and ṗ=−aq.
  2. Its derivative is Ḣθ=aq·bp+bp·(−aq)=0.
  3. For positive a,b, the frequency is √(ab); wrong coefficients can preserve the learned energy perfectly while predicting the wrong frequency.

When it applies

A basic conservative HNN does not describe damping or forcing without extension. Continuous structural properties also need compatible numerical integration.

Check your understanding

Does a flat energy trace prove that the model has learned the correct oscillation?

No. Conservation and frequency accuracy test different properties.
