Spots, stripes and mazes

Can a uniform-looking surface develop a pattern without a designer drawing it?

Run this experiment →

The idea

Two spatial fields interact locally and spread at different rates. Local activation and a slower or faster response compete with smoothing by diffusion. A disturbance can grow at some wavelengths while other wavelengths decay. The picture emerges from repeated updates, not from a stored image.

A pattern's spacing is a question about unstable spatial modes. Its final shape also depends on nonlinear saturation, the domain and the initial disturbance.

Read the relationship

uₜ=0.1(u−u³−v+b)+0.16Δu vₜ=0.2(u−0.8v)+DᵥΔv

u and v are signed activator/inhibitor deviations, not necessarily nonnegative chemical concentrations. Dᵥ sets the inhibitor's diffusion rate.

Try this

  1. Start from weak random variation. Change the diffusion ratio, reset, and compare whether the emerging structure is fine, broad or nearly uniform.

When it applies

This is a dimensionless teaching model with periodic boundaries. An attractive pattern alone does not establish a Turing instability in a real chemical system.
