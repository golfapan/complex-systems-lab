Vectors, Linear Combinations and Span

With a few available directions, which destinations can you reach?

The idea

A vector can represent a displacement, a list of measurements, or coefficients describing a function. Linear algebra begins with two operations: scaling vectors and adding them. A linear combination records how much of each available direction to use; the span is the complete set of outputs those combinations can produce.

Independence asks whether a direction is redundant. If one vector can be built from the others, it adds no new freedom. An independent spanning set is a basis, so every vector in the space has unique coordinates in that set. Coordinates describe an object relative to chosen directions; they are not the object itself.

Read the relationship

y=x₁a₁+…+xₙaₙ=Ax Independence: Σxᵢaᵢ=0 implies every xᵢ=0

The columns aᵢ of A are available directions; x contains their weights. A combination may use positive, negative or zero weights.

Work through an example

  1. Let a₁=(1,0) and a₂=(1,1), and seek b=(3,2).
  2. The second coordinate forces x₂=2; the first then gives x₁+x₂=3, so x₁=1.
  3. Thus b=a₁+2a₂. These two independent directions span the entire plane.

When it applies

The number of recorded features need not equal the data's effective dimension. Many coordinates may carry redundant information.

Check your understanding

Do (1,0), (0,1) and (1,1) form a basis of the plane?

They span the plane, but are dependent because the third is the sum of the first two. A basis must satisfy both requirements.
