Time series, dependence, and forecasting

How do you evaluate predictions when the future is not yet available?

The idea

Time series retain order because nearby observations may share information, and their distributions can change. Forecasting must mimic the information available at each decision time. A rolling evaluation repeatedly fits using the past and scores predictions of later observations, matching the intended forecast horizon.

An autoregressive model provides a small example of dependence and mean reversion. Its stationary formulas require the relevant conditions; trends, seasonal patterns, and unit roots may need another description. One-step forecasts using the latest real observation also differ from multistep forecasts that recursively use earlier predictions.

Read the relationship

Xt=c+φXt−1+εt; stationary mean μ=c/(1−φ) when |φ|<1 in this causal AR(1) model

εt is independent zero-mean noise. Dependence changes uncertainty estimates and the effective information in a sequence.

Work through an example

  1. Take c=2, φ=0.8, and the current value Xt=15.
  2. The stationary mean is 2/(1−0.8)=10.
  3. The next conditional-mean forecast is 2+0.8×15=14, closer to ten than the current value.

When it applies

Do not fit preprocessing using future values or randomly shuffle a forecasting problem without justification. The mean formula is invalid at φ=1.

Check your understanding

With c=0 and φ=0.5, what are one- and two-step forecasts from eight?

They are four and two, since future innovations have conditional mean zero.
