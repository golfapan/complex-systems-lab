Waves in an excitable medium

Why can one disturbance trigger a travelling wave, followed by a quiet recovery period?

Run this experiment →

The idea

A fast activator responds strongly once a local threshold is crossed. A slower recovery variable temporarily prevents the same region from firing again. Diffusion carries activation into neighbouring regions, allowing a wave to travel through otherwise resting material.

Excitability and oscillation are different: a resting region may need a sufficiently strong trigger rather than oscillating by itself.

Read the relationship

uₜ=Δu+u(1−u)[u−(v+0.01)/0.75]/0.02 vₜ=u−v

u is the fast activator and v the slower recovery variable. This is a particular dimensionless excitable model.

Try this

  1. Restart the broken-wave seed and watch the tip rotate. Compare the moving wavefront with the region behind it that is still recovering.

When it applies

This is a qualitative teaching model, not a calibrated heart or neuron simulation. Its wave dynamics should not be equated with every reaction–diffusion pattern.
