Flow Matching and Continuous Normalising Flows

Can a model learn how to move noise towards data directly?

The idea

Flow Matching designs conditional paths between easy samples and data samples, then trains a network against their target velocities. After training, generation integrates the learned velocity field. Simulation-free training refers to constructing the training objective without repeatedly solving the current model's complete ODE.

A conditional target can depend on both endpoints, while the learned field sees only the current location, time and supplied conditions. Under squared loss, averaging compatible conditional velocities connects the training pairs to a population-level transport field. That is more than memorising a single straight line.

Read the relationship

Xₜ=(1−t)X₀+tX₁; Uₜ=X₁−X₀; minimise E‖vθ(Xₜ,t)−Uₜ‖²

t runs from zero to one. X₀ comes from the base distribution and X₁ from data; their coupling is part of the path design.

Work through an example

  1. Pair X₀=−1 with X₁=3. At t=0.25 the interpolated position is zero.
  2. The target velocity at that training point is four.
  3. Another endpoint pair may reach the same location and time with a different velocity; the field learns an appropriate conditional average.

When it applies

Independent pairings are not automatically globally optimal transport. Straight conditional paths do not imply every learned marginal trajectory is straight, and sampling still incurs numerical error.

Check your understanding

Does simulation-free training mean standard sampling requires no integration?

No. The learned continuous flow is normally integrated during generation.
