From Continuous Models to Discrete Computation

What changes when a field becomes a finite array?

The idea

Spatial discretisation replaces a function with grid values or basis coefficients. It can turn a PDE into a large ODE or algebraic system. Time discretisation then specifies how that finite state advances. Their errors should be examined separately before being combined.

Finite differences approximate derivatives, finite volumes emphasise balances, finite elements use trial and test spaces, and spectral methods use global modes. The method of lines discretises space first while retaining continuous time. Each choice still has to respect boundaries and the solution's regularity.

Read the relationship

uₜ=κuₓₓ → U̇ⱼ=κ(Uⱼ₊₁−2Uⱼ+Uⱼ₋₁)/Δx²; Euler requires κΔt/Δx²≤1/2

Uⱼ approximates the field at a grid point. The stated limit applies to this one-dimensional centred spatial stencil with explicit Euler.

Work through an example

  1. With κ=1 and Δx=0.1, the stated stability condition gives Δt≤0.005.
  2. Halving Δx to 0.05 reduces the limit to 0.00125.
  3. Doubling spatial resolution therefore also increases the required number of explicit time steps by a factor of four.

When it applies

The numerical constant is not universal. Stability does not establish accuracy, and implicit integration can relax stability restrictions while still needing adequate temporal resolution.

Check your understanding

Can any large implicit time step be assumed accurate?

No. Stability, truncation error and the accuracy of the implicit solve remain separate checks.
