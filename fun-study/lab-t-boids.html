Complex Systems Lab / 04

Flocking without a leader

How can a flock turn together when nobody sees the whole flock?

Run this experiment →

The idea

A boid combines three local steering preferences: avoid close neighbours, align with nearby headings, and move towards the local group. These preferences need not agree. Their weights, sensing radius and turn limits determine what the group can do together.

The group-level order parameter measures heading agreement, not intelligence or physical realism. Each boid still uses only a local neighbourhood.

Read the relationship

steering=wₛ separation+wₐ alignment+w꜀ cohesion x⁺=x+Δt v⁺

Separation depends on relative positions; alignment compares velocities; cohesion points towards neighbouring positions. They are not three names for one force.

Try this

  1. Turn down alignment while keeping separation. Then turn down separation while keeping cohesion. Compare loose disorder, crowding and coordinated motion.

When it applies

The model caps steering and fixes speed. Its short-range separation term is a behavioural rule, not an electrostatic inverse-square force. Its displacement/r² separation term has 1/r magnitude before steering normalization.
