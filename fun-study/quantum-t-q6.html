Fourier structure and uncertainty

Why does localizing a wavepacket broaden its momentum distribution?

The idea

Position and momentum are connected by a Fourier transformation. A narrow wavepacket needs a broad range of spatial frequencies to build its sharp shape, and momentum is related to those frequencies. The uncertainty relation makes that tradeoff quantitative using standard deviations within the same prepared state.

It is not a claim that every individual position reading is vague, nor that careless instruments create the entire effect. Repeating position measurements and repeating momentum measurements on equivalently prepared systems reveal two distributions whose spreads cannot both be made arbitrarily small. Measurement disturbance is a related but separate question.

Read the relationship

[x̂,p̂]=iħ; ΔxΔp≥ħ/2; for a minimum-uncertainty Gaussian, Δp=ħ/(2Δx)

Δ denotes a state's standard deviation, not an arbitrary instrument tolerance or a difference between two recorded values.

Work through an example

  1. Take a Gaussian wavepacket with position standard deviation σ.
  2. Its minimum compatible momentum spread is ħ/(2σ).
  3. Halving the position spread doubles this momentum spread, leaving the product ħ/2 in this special minimum-uncertainty family.

When it applies

Many states have a larger uncertainty product. Boundary conditions, operator domains, and the chosen observables matter when applying commutator arguments.

Check your understanding

Does a precise position record contradict ΔxΔp≥ħ/2?

No. A single result is not the same object as the standard deviation of the preparation's outcome distribution.
