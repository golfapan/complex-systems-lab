State Vectors, Matrix Exponentials and Phase Portraits

What information is enough to predict the next stage of motion?

The idea

A position alone is insufficient for a second-order mechanical model: the same position with opposite velocities has different futures. Collecting position and velocity into a state turns a high-order scalar equation into a first-order vector system. The state space describes possible internal conditions, not necessarily physical room coordinates.

For a constant matrix A, the matrix exponential advances every initial state. Eigenvectors reveal directions with simple exponential behaviour when enough independent eigenvectors exist; the matrix exponential also handles defective matrices. Negative real parts guarantee eventual decay, but nonorthogonal modes can still create substantial temporary amplification.

Read the relationship

x′=Ax ⇒ x(t)=e^(At)x₀ e^(At)=I+At+(At)²/2!+… All Re λ(A)<0 ⇔ asymptotic stability at the origin

x is a state vector and A a constant dynamics matrix. A phase portrait compares state trajectories from different initial values.

Work through an example

  1. Take A=[[-1,10],[0,-2]] and x(0)=(0,1).
  2. Then x₂=e⁻²ᵗ and x₁=10(e⁻ᵗ−e⁻²ᵗ).
  3. At t=ln2, x₁=2.5, larger than the original state norm, although both components eventually decay to zero.

When it applies

Eigenvalues on the stability boundary require additional analysis, including Jordan structure. For variable matrices A(t), exp(∫A dt) is not generally the correct solution.

Check your understanding

Does pure rotation count as asymptotically stable?

No. Nearby trajectories remain nearby, but they keep circling rather than converging to the origin; that is stability without asymptotic attraction.
