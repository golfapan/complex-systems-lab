Exponential Modes and Damped Oscillation

Why do exponentials appear so often in linear differential equations?

The idea

Differentiating an exponential changes its multiplier but not its shape. A constant-coefficient differential operator therefore acts on eʳᵗ like a polynomial evaluated at r. Setting that polynomial to zero identifies the system's natural modes and converts a differential problem into algebra.

Real parts of characteristic roots control growth or decay; imaginary parts control oscillation. A repeated root needs an additional independent solution, often teʳᵗ, rather than simply writing the same exponential twice. Initial position and velocity determine how the available modes are mixed, not which characteristic roots the system has.

Read the relationship

my″+by′+ky=0 ⇒ mr²+br+k=0 r=α±iβ ⇒ y=eᵅᵗ(Ccosβt+Dsinβt) Repeated r: y=(C+Dt)eʳᵗ

m, b and k represent mass, damping and stiffness in a mechanical example. C and D are fixed by two initial conditions.

Work through an example

  1. For y″+2y′+5y=0, the characteristic polynomial is r²+2r+5.
  2. Its roots are −1±2i.
  3. Thus y=e⁻ᵗ(Ccos2t+Dsin2t): a decaying envelope with angular oscillation frequency 2.

When it applies

This characteristic-polynomial method assumes constant coefficients. Suitable variable-coefficient linear equations still have a solution space, but generally require other tools.

Check your understanding

For the unforced mechanical equation, what does E′=−b(y′)² tell us?

With E=my′²/2+ky²/2 and b≥0, mechanical energy cannot increase. This structural result does not require the explicit solution.
