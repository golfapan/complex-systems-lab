Data-Driven Dynamics: SINDy, DMD and Hybrid Models

Which model should you recover from measurements?

The idea

SINDy seeks a sparse combination of candidate functions, aiming for a compact explicit equation. DMD fits a finite linear step between snapshots or observables, often to extract modes and timescales. Both use data, but they return different mathematical objects.

A hybrid model preserves trusted mechanisms and learns a residual for what is unknown. That can use data efficiently, but an incorrect trusted component can also limit the fit. Choose a method according to whether the aim is interpretation, prediction, compression or control, then test the corresponding evidence.

Read the relationship

SINDy: Ẋ≈Θ(X)Ξ; DMD: X′≈AX; hybrid: ẋ=fknown(x,u)+rθ(x,u)

rθ denotes an unknown-mechanism correction, not numerical truncation residual. A advances a sampled state; Ξ weights candidate continuous-rate terms.

Work through an example

  1. For ẋ=−2x sampled every 0.1 seconds, ideal SINDy identifies the rate coefficient −2.
  2. Ideal DMD instead identifies the multiplier e⁻⁰·²≈0.81873.
  3. Treating 0.81873 as the continuous derivative coefficient confuses a finite-time map with a generator.

When it applies

Sparse does not establish causality; a DMD mode is not automatically a physical global mode. Hidden variables, noise and training range all affect recovery.

Check your understanding

Can low one-step prediction error establish suitability for long-horizon control?

No. Check rolling error, constraints, stability and closed-loop performance on independent cases.
