Relativity / 06

Four-momentum, mass, and energy

How do energy and momentum become one geometric object?

The idea

Relativity combines energy and spatial momentum into a four-vector. Observers can assign different energy and momentum components while agreeing on its invariant norm. For a particle with nonzero rest mass, the resulting relationship connects relativistic motion smoothly to the familiar Newtonian kinetic-energy approximation at low speed.

Rest mass is not a frame-dependent amount that grows because a particle moves. Keeping invariant mass separate from frame-dependent energy avoids confusing terminology. For a photon, the invariant mass is zero even though its energy and momentum need not vanish. A zero norm in Minkowski geometry does not require a zero vector.

Read the relationship

P=(E/c,p); E²−c²|p|²=m²c⁴; E=γmc²; p=γmv; photon: E=c|p|

m is invariant rest mass. The formulas with γ apply to a massive particle's speed; a photon uses the null relationship instead.

Work through an example

  1. Use c=1, m=1, and v=0.6. Then γ=1.25.
  2. Energy is 1.25 and momentum is 0.75.
  3. Their invariant combination is 1.25²−0.75²=1, recovering m² even though both components depend on frame.

When it applies

A system's invariant mass includes its total internal energy and momentum balance. Do not apply a single-particle formula blindly to a composite system.

Check your understanding

Does a massless photon have zero energy?

No. It can have positive energy with E=c|p| while its invariant mass remains zero.
