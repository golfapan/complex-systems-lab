Scientific Machine Learning and Embodied AI

What changes when predictions must respect physics or drive a real action?

The idea

Scientific machine learning may learn a solution function, a solution operator or a dynamical rule. Embodied AI adds sensing, policy, action interfaces and physical feedback. These outputs have different mathematical roles and need different validation.

Known structure can guide learning, but conservation, extrapolation, stability and hardware limits remain separate questions. An offline predictor may look accurate on recorded trajectories while failing after its actions change the state distribution. Connect the learned object to the actual deployment loop before choosing evidence.

Read the relationship

solution uθ(x,t) ≠ operator Gθ:a↦u ≠ feedback policy πθ(o)

Coordinates index a solution; a function or condition indexes an operator's problem instance; observations o inform a policy's action.

Work through an example

  1. A PINN can fit small residuals on its sampled points.
  2. A new boundary condition tests a different problem instance and may expose a failure.
  3. Likewise, good recorded robot-action prediction does not establish successful contact manipulation in a closed loop.

When it applies

Simulation, offline fit and physical deployment support different claims. Verify the required target rather than transferring confidence by analogy.

Check your understanding

Why is an accurate dynamics model not automatically a successful controller?

Prediction does not by itself choose feasible, robust actions under the task's objectives and constraints.
