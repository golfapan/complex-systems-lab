Generalisation and Overfitting

Why can memorising training examples fail on new ones?

The idea

Generalisation concerns fresh samples from a target distribution. Training risk estimates performance using the observed dataset; population risk averages over the target source. Their difference depends on data, model capacity, noise and the learning procedure, not simply on depth.

Training and validation curves can reveal a widening gap, but they do not uniquely diagnose its cause. Distribution shift asks an additional question: does performance survive a new setting? Related images, people or devices can leak across a random split and make apparent generalisation much easier than the intended task.

Read the relationship

R̂(θ)=(1/N)Σᵢℓᵢ; R(θ)=E[ℓ]

R̂ is empirical risk and R expected target risk. Their relationship depends on how the data were sampled and what target use is intended.

Work through an example

  1. Randomly split adjacent frames from one video into training and test sets.
  2. A model may recognise the shared scene and score highly without learning robust object behaviour.
  3. Test on another day or camera and the result may collapse; a grouped split would expose that risk earlier.

When it applies

A single validation curve cannot explain every failure. Test-set reuse and repeated model selection can also bias reported performance.

Check your understanding

Is poor performance on a new camera necessarily proof that the network is too small?

No. Sampling, distribution shift, preprocessing and shortcut features are alternative explanations.
